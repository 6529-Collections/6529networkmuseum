The following is the adopted proposal text.

The 6529 Network Museum considers historically, artistically, culturally, or technically significant NFTs and blockchain-native digital objects to be within scope for the Museum's collection.

This scope includes, without limitation:

1. **PFPs and avatar projects**

   Collections that contributed meaningfully to digital identity, community formation, avatar culture, online self-representation, or the evolution of NFT-native collecting.

2. **Generative art**

   Algorithmic, procedural, on-chain, long-form, or otherwise system-based digital artworks, including both fully on-chain and partially on-chain works.

3. **1/1 digital art**

   Unique digital artworks issued as NFTs, including works by artists using NFTs as a native medium, distribution mechanism, provenance layer, or market structure.

4. **Editions**

   Limited, open, timed, or otherwise editioned NFT artworks, including works where the edition structure itself is part of the artistic, cultural, or distribution logic.

5. **NFT photography**

   Photographic works issued as NFTs, including both single-image works and editioned photographic projects, where the NFT meaningfully contributes to provenance, distribution, community, or artistic context.

6. **Meme-native and internet-culture works**

   NFTs and blockchain-native objects rooted in memes, remix culture, networked communities, viral images, internet-native symbols, or shared cultural references.

7. **Interactive, dynamic, and programmable works**

   NFTs that change over time, respond to data, include interactive elements, depend on smart contracts, or otherwise use blockchain infrastructure as part of the work.

8. **AI, computational, and software-based works**

   Works created with, about, or through artificial intelligence, code, software systems, autonomous processes, or computational media.

9. **Video, audio, literary, and mixed-media NFTs**

   NFT-native works using moving image, sound, music, poetry, text, performance documentation, or hybrid digital media.

10. **Virtual-world, gaming, and metaverse-native objects**

    Digital objects, wearables, land, environments, game assets, or artifacts that are historically or culturally significant to open metaverse development, provided they have sufficient curatorial relevance.

11. **Protocol-native and platform-native cultural artifacts**

    Blockchain-native objects that may not fit traditional art categories but are important to the history of NFTs, digital ownership, crypto culture, open networks, or the development of decentralized cultural systems.

The inclusion of a category within scope does not mean that every work or collection in that category must be accepted. Individual works, collections, and acquisition programs remain subject to the Museum's ordinary procedures for curatorial review, provenance, title, authenticity, custody, wallet security, legal compliance, donor acceptance, budgeting, and governance.

The Museum may also establish specific safe-harbor categories or pre-approved collections from time to time. Where such a safe harbor has been approved, donations from that collection may be accepted without further collection-specific review, while still remaining subject to the Museum's ordinary operational, legal, provenance, and custody checks.

Acquisition programs involving NFTs or blockchain-native digital objects may be pursued, provided they are approved, funded, and executed through the Museum's ordinary governance, budgeting, and acquisition processes.

## Rationale

The NFT field is broader than any single format. Some of its most important works are generative art projects; others are PFPs, 1/1s, editions, photographs, memes, dynamic works, virtual-world objects, or artifacts that are difficult to classify using older museum categories.

The 6529 Network Museum should therefore define its collecting scope broadly enough to recognize the full range of blockchain-native culture. NFTs are not merely a wrapper for digital images. In many cases, the token, contract, provenance, community, minting structure, edition logic, ownership history, and network context are part of the work's meaning.

At the same time, a broad collecting scope should not become automatic approval for every NFT project. The better structure is to distinguish between category-level eligibility and collection-level approval. Broad categories can be in scope, while specific works and collections remain subject to review unless separately approved as safe harbors.

This approach gives the Museum flexibility, intellectual seriousness, and operational discipline. It allows the Museum to collect across the full history of NFT-native culture while preserving clear standards for acceptance, acquisition, and long-term stewardship.
