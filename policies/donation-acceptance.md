## 1. Purpose

The 6529 Network Museum may accept donations of digital artworks, NFTs, on-chain cultural artifacts, and related works that are consistent with its mission and collecting scope.

This policy establishes two donation pathways:

1. donations from preapproved collections, which may be accepted without further pre-authorization; and
2. all other donations, which require authorization before acceptance.

The purpose of this policy is to make clearly eligible donations easy to accept while preserving appropriate review for works that have not already been approved as part of a recognized collection or category.

## 2. Core principle

A work is not part of the Museum’s collection merely because it has been offered, proposed, transferred, airdropped, or sent to a Museum-associated wallet.

A work becomes part of the Museum’s collection only when it is accepted under this policy or another valid Museum governance process.

## 3. Preapproved collections

The Museum may, from time to time, approve certain collections or categories as preapproved for donation purposes.

Donations from preapproved collections do not require further collection-specific pre-authorization before acceptance.

Preapproved status means that the collection or category has already been determined to be within the Museum’s scope. It does not waive ordinary donation checks, including authenticity, provenance, title, donor authority, legal compliance, rights, technical receivability, and donor acceptance.

The list of preapproved collections should be maintained separately from this policy and may be amended from time to time through the Museum’s ordinary governance process.

## 4. Donations from preapproved collections

A donor may offer a work from a preapproved collection without obtaining prior collection-specific authorization.

The Museum may accept the donation administratively, provided that:

1. the work is authentic and belongs to the relevant preapproved collection;
2. the donor appears to have valid title, authority, or control;
3. the work is not subject to known disputes, theft claims, sanctions concerns, liens, encumbrances, or credible adverse provenance issues;
4. the Museum is legally and technically able to receive the work;
5. the donation does not impose unacceptable obligations, restrictions, costs, or risks;
6. the donation is otherwise consistent with the Museum’s mission and policies.

If these conditions are not satisfied, the Museum may refuse the donation or require further review before acceptance.

## 5. Donations outside preapproved collections

All donations outside preapproved collections require authorization before acceptance.

This includes works from non-preapproved NFT collections, 1/1 works, editions, photography, AI art, generative art outside preapproved categories, meme-based works outside preapproved categories, PFP collections outside preapproved categories, video, sound, software-based works, interactive works, metaverse objects, physical-digital works, and other digital cultural objects.

Authorization may be granted through the Museum’s ordinary governance process or through a delegated donation review process approved by Museum governance.

Until authorization is granted, the work should be treated as a proposed donation, not as an accepted collection work.

## 6. Review criteria for non-preapproved donations

For donations requiring authorization, the Museum should consider:

1. artistic merit;
2. historical importance;
3. cultural significance;
4. relevance to NFTs, open metaverse culture, memes, digital ownership, decentralized networks, blockchain-native art, or internet-native culture;
5. relationship to the 6529 ecosystem or to the broader cultural history that informs it;
6. importance of the artist, project, collection, community, or technical format;
7. provenance and authenticity;
8. rights, licenses, and display permissions;
9. rarity, edition structure, metadata, and chain of custody;
10. technical durability and preservation risk;
11. legal, sanctions, reputational, and compliance risk;
12. fit with the Museum’s existing collection;
13. whether the work fills a meaningful gap in the collection;
14. whether accepting the work would impose unreasonable obligations on the Museum.

The Museum should not accept a donation merely because it is free. A donated work should still meet the Museum’s standards for relevance, quality, integrity, and long-term responsibility.

## 7. Unsolicited transfers

Unsolicited transfers, airdrops, spam tokens, scam tokens, malicious tokens, or accidental transfers are not automatically accepted as donations.

The Museum may ignore, reject, quarantine, return, burn, transfer, hide, or otherwise dispose of unsolicited or unwanted assets where legally and technically appropriate.

The Museum should not be deemed to have accepted a work solely because the work was transferred to a Museum-associated wallet or address.

## 8. Conditional and restricted donations

The Museum may accept conditional or restricted donations only if the conditions are clear, lawful, manageable, and consistent with the Museum’s mission.

Restrictions requiring review include:

1. permanent display requirements;
2. resale or non-resale restrictions;
3. naming rights;
4. mandatory donor recognition;
5. requirements to promote the donor, artist, project, collection, or market;
6. restrictions on interpretation, criticism, scholarship, or display context;
7. obligations to acquire, hold, sell, or support related works;
8. obligations to maintain a specific website, display environment, software environment, or technical dependency;
9. any promise or implication regarding valuation, tax treatment, liquidity, market support, or floor-price support.

The Museum should not accept donations that compromise curatorial independence.

## 9. Rights and display

Donation of an NFT or other digital object does not necessarily transfer copyright, commercial rights, reproduction rights, display rights, or preservation rights.

Before accepting a donation, the Museum should record, where reasonably available:

1. what rights are transferred;
2. what rights remain with the artist, donor, platform, or third party;
3. whether the Museum may publicly display the work;
4. whether the Museum may reproduce the work for cataloging, education, research, publicity, archival, or preservation purposes;
5. whether the Museum may preserve, back up, mirror, migrate, or otherwise safeguard associated media and metadata;
6. any restrictions on use, display, publication, reproduction, or interpretation.

Where rights are unclear, the Museum may still accept the work, but the uncertainty should be recorded.

## 10. Provenance and authenticity

Before accepting a donation, the Museum should make reasonable efforts to confirm:

1. the correct collection, contract, token, edition, or object identity;
2. the authenticity of the work;
3. the donor’s authority to donate the work;
4. the relevant ownership or transfer history;
5. the absence of known disputes, theft claims, sanctions issues, or other adverse provenance concerns;
6. the accuracy and stability of relevant metadata and media references;
7. any unusual technical, legal, or preservation risks.

For high-value, disputed, complex, or historically important works, the Museum may require enhanced review.

## 11. Donor recognition

The Museum may recognize donors in appropriate ways, subject to donor consent and Museum policy.

Donor recognition does not give the donor control over interpretation, display, future collection decisions, governance, or the Museum’s treatment of the work.

Anonymous donations may be accepted, provided that the Museum can conduct any diligence it considers necessary.

## 12. Valuation and tax

The Museum should not provide donors with tax, accounting, legal, or valuation advice.

The Museum may acknowledge receipt of an accepted donation, but it should not guarantee or represent any particular value, tax treatment, deductibility, or legal characterization.

Any valuation used for internal purposes should be treated as an internal Museum record and not as advice to the donor.

## 13. Refusal of donations

The Museum may refuse any proposed donation, including a donation from a preapproved collection, if acceptance would be inconsistent with the Museum’s mission, policies, legal obligations, curatorial standards, preservation responsibilities, or public trust.

The Museum may also decline a donation if it creates unacceptable legal, technical, reputational, financial, administrative, or governance risk.

## 14. Records of accepted donations

For each accepted donation, the Museum should maintain an appropriate record, which may include:

1. donor information, where available and appropriate;
2. date of acceptance;
3. collection or artist name;
4. title or token identifier;
5. blockchain, contract address, token ID, edition number, or equivalent identifier;
6. transaction hash or other transfer evidence;
7. rights and license information;
8. provenance notes;
9. acceptance pathway, including whether the donation was accepted as part of a preapproved collection or through separate authorization;
10. any restrictions, conditions, or special considerations.

## 15. Summary

This policy establishes a focused donation framework for the 6529 Network Museum:

1. donations from preapproved collections may be accepted without further pre-authorization;
2. preapproved collections are approved from time to time outside this policy;
3. all other donations require authorization before acceptance;
4. unsolicited transfers are not automatically accepted;
5. every accepted donation remains subject to appropriate checks for authenticity, provenance, rights, legality, donor authority, and mission fit.

The result is a donation policy that is open enough for a decentralized network museum, but disciplined enough for a serious cultural institution.
