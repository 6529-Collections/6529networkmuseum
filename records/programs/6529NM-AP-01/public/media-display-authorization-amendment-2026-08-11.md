The Museum authorizes the public presentation of the sixteen images selected
through Keys and Gates. The images are shown so that visitors can encounter
the program as a work of curation, read the photographs together, and follow
the artists and individual work records.

This authorization governs display. It does not complete the planned mint or
purchase, transfer title or custody, activate the program's prospective CC0
terms, admit a work to the permanent Collection, or create a public download
right. The current label remains: **selected through Keys and Gates;
acquisition and minting pending**.

## Presentation set

The active presentation set is the exact group recorded in
`selected-works.json` and `media-manifest.json`. Each image is a deterministic
WebP surrogate made from the artist's submitted source. The manifest binds the
source observation, transform profile, dimensions, file size, SHA-256 digest,
repository path, delivery URL, and visual description for every released
surrogate.

Fourteen works may use the 640, 1280, and 2400 pixel widths. OUT-004 and
OUT-011 use the 640 pixel surrogate only. Those limits retain the prior
work-specific editorial disposition for images involving children described
by the submitting artist or nudity with an unresolved consent instrument.
The limitation is a publication measure, not a finding about identity, age,
consent, or any undisclosed fact.

## Conditions of display

- Images appear only in the Keys and Gates program, curated acquisition,
  artist, and work contexts.
- Every presentation carries the recorded artist, title, program status, and
  rights boundary.
- Images are shown without cropping and without a download, IIIF, or
  preservation-master claim.
- The website does not identify depicted people beyond the public record and
  does not infer age, consent, biography, location, or circumstance.
- The submitted source and the web surrogate remain distinct. The source is
  not offered to visitors.
- A later rights, consent, title, mint, custody, accession, or preservation
  record may add facts. None is inferred here.
