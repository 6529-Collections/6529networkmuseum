# Rights for collectors

Collecting a digital artwork brings together ownership, access, evidence, and
care. The token may be easy to see on a blockchain. The rights needed to show,
publish, preserve, lend, or donate the associated work often live in metadata,
platform terms, a public license, or a separate agreement.

A collector protects the work by keeping those layers together.

## Ownership and copyright are different

Buying a token or physical artwork usually gives the collector ownership of
that object. Copyright usually stays with the artist unless an effective
transfer says otherwise. This is ordinary in museum collections: an
institution can own a painting while reproduction rights remain with the
artist or estate.

The distinction does not diminish the collection object. Ownership can carry
authenticity, scarcity, provenance, access, display possibilities, and the
right to transfer the object. Copyright controls a separate set of acts, such
as reproducing the image, publishing an adaptation, or authorizing another
person to do so.

## The public domain is part of everyday collecting

Copyright expires. Much of the best-known art in the world is consequently in
the public domain: works by Leonardo, Vermeer, Hokusai, and Van Gogh can be
reproduced and reinterpreted without permission from a copyright owner. This
is one reason they can circulate so widely in scholarship and public life.

Public-domain status should be established from the artist, dates,
publication history, and relevant jurisdictions. A particular scan,
photograph, edition, or website may have separate terms. Once those layers are
identified, public-domain material is a durable and useful part of culture,
rather than a rights category to fear.

## What a token purchase usually gives you

The blockchain records ownership of the token under the contract's rules. The
token may point to media, identify an edition, or carry part of the work
on-chain. The purchase may also bring contractual rights stated by the artist
or platform.

Copyright in the artwork is separate. A collector receives copyright or an
exclusive copyright right only through an effective transfer. A public license
such as CC BY or CC BY-NC is available to the collector on the same terms as
everyone else. Holder terms may add rights tied specifically to possession of
the token.

## Read the rights before you rely on them

Find the exact wording that applied when the token was minted or sold. Look in:

- on-chain metadata and contract references;
- the token's metadata response and media files;
- the artist's canonical project page;
- platform terms and project-specific terms;
- a signed purchase, commission, or transfer agreement;
- a Creative Commons URI or custom license linked from the work.

Save exact copies with dates and source URLs. Platform pages and metadata
gateways can change. A screenshot is useful context; retained bytes, hashes,
and transaction records provide stronger evidence.

## Display

Personal display in a home or on a device may be addressed by holder terms.
Public display in a gallery, office, event, website, or social feed can involve
copyright rights and platform conditions. Check whether the applicable public
or holder license covers the setting, whether the use is commercial, and
whether a live generator depends on a third-party service.

Credit the artist and retain the title, date, edition, project, license, and
change information. Good labels strengthen the work's history even when the
license does not legally require every field.

## Posting and publishing

Owning the token does not automatically permit posting a full-resolution image,
making prints, publishing a catalogue, licensing an image, or using the work in
advertising. The public license or holder terms may permit some or all of those
uses.

For Creative Commons material, follow the exact conditions. BY requires credit
and notices. NC limits the public grant to noncommercial uses. ND withholds
permission to share adaptations. SA applies the same or compatible license to
shared adaptations.

Linking to an official Museum or artist page usually avoids making and
distributing another copy of the artwork, while still directing viewers to the
work and its context.

## Preservation

Keep more than the marketplace image. A useful preservation package may
include:

- contract address, token ID, chain, and mint transaction;
- token metadata and its retrieval date;
- media files and content hashes;
- source code or an artist-authorized preservation copy, when available;
- dependency versions, fonts, libraries, and external assets;
- display instructions and a recording of the work operating;
- the exact license, holder terms, and platform terms;
- correspondence that clarifies authenticity, installation, or rights.

Check whether the work can run without a platform, API, hosted script, or web
domain. Record what happens when an external service disappears. Preservation
rights matter most when technical access becomes difficult.

## Lending and exhibition

A loan should identify the token, the files supplied, the authorized display
version, the exhibition period, the venue, the equipment or environment, the
credit line, insurance and security responsibilities, and permitted publicity.
It should also say what the borrower may copy and what must be returned or
deleted.

If the work is interactive, generative, or time-based, include installation
instructions and the expected behavior. Record any approved substitution,
migration, or emulation made for the exhibition.

## Donating a work

A complete gift gives the receiving museum the token and every transferable
interest the donor actually holds. It cannot give rights that remain with the
artist or another licensor.

Prepare:

1. the wallet and transaction history;
2. evidence of lawful title and any liens, delegations, or approvals;
3. the work and preservation package;
4. the operative public license, holder terms, and platform terms;
5. any direct agreement with the artist;
6. exhibition history, publications, and conservation events;
7. a proposed public credit line;
8. a signed gift instrument identifying the token and files transferred.

The receiving museum should make its own title, rights, technical, condition,
and curatorial determinations. A transfer into the museum wallet records
custody. Formal acceptance and accession complete the institutional record.

## Resale and transfer

Before a sale or gift, check whether holder-specific rights follow the token,
end on transfer, or require an additional step. Transfer the documentation and
preservation files through a secure channel. Do not place private keys or
sensitive personal records in the public provenance package.

On-chain royalties, marketplace royalties, contractual resale payments, and
statutory artist resale rights are separate mechanisms. Their operation depends
on the contract, marketplace, agreement, and jurisdiction.

## A collector's rights checklist

- Can I identify the artist or other licensor?
- Which components does the license cover?
- Is the license public, holder-specific, custom, or absent?
- May I display the work publicly and online?
- May I post a copy or make a print?
- May I make preservation copies or migrate the work?
- May I publish a crop, translation, or other adaptation?
- Does commercial use require separate permission?
- What credit, notices, links, and change statements are required?
- Which terms and files need to pass to the next owner?

An unresolved answer belongs in the record. Guessing from a marketplace badge
or from token ownership can create a false history that becomes harder to
correct with each transfer.
