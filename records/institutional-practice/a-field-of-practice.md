## Overview

An encounter with [Douglas Davis's *The World's First Collaborative Sentence*](profiles/whitney.md)
can involve a live contribution surface, a restored historical state, broken
links, and malformed Korean characters. Rhizome's record of [*My Boyfriend Came
Back From the War*](profiles/rhizome-new-museum.md) asks a related question at
the level of the browser: nested frames divide the screen, clicking changes the
composition, and variant history records the access environment. These cases
establish the study's starting point: a digital artwork
requires a record of what it does, where it runs, which state a reader
encounters, and what changed during care.

The study follows four connected questions:

1. How is a digital work identified when its behavior depends on a runtime,
   browser, network, device, or service?
2. Which changes count as intervention, migration, reconstruction, or loss?
3. How can a collection record open paths to research, correction, citation,
   and reuse?
4. Which parts of a method depend on specific labor, rights, budgets, or
   technical infrastructure?

The profiles follow the operational questions they answer. The order
establishes a reading path; it carries no claim about reputation, scale,
quality, chronology, or comparative rank.

## Selection method

The study comprises twenty-seven case files. Selection is case-based. A profile enters
when public primary institutional material documents a work, project, record,
archive, conservation action, technical tool, commission, or research
publication that bears directly on digital art or on a transferable museum
record problem.

Each case must provide:

- a named anchor work, project, record, or institutional artifact;
- a concrete action or record structure that a reader can inspect;
- a documented limit, dependency, omission, unresolved state, or change
  history;
- exact source titles, dates, access information, and claim-level links; and
- a reason the evidence matters to the Museum's collection, publication,
  preservation, rights, or public-access work.

Mission statements establish stated purpose. Collection records, work pages,
archives, conservation studies, datasets, technical documentation, research
publications, and program records establish demonstrated practice. A profile
may therefore describe an ongoing or partial method without presenting it as a
completed institutional capability.

The study excludes prestige, collection size, visitor numbers, awards,
marketplace metrics, generic technology claims, and institutional promotion as
selection grounds. Broad institutional history appears only when it explains a
specific record, work, decision, or dependency. No profile is an endorsement of
the institution as a whole.

## Working lessons

### Describe the encounter before the interpretation

Every digital-work record should state what the work does and what the reader
must use to encounter it: interface, runtime, browser or device, network,
interaction, duration, sound, display state, and known access limits.

### Separate identity, manifestation, and intervention

Keep a stable work identity beside dated manifestations and intervention
events. A live version, historical capture, emulation, migration, restoration,
and static documentation surrogate should remain distinguishable.

### Publish loss and missingness

Record unavailable dependencies, broken links, incomplete captures, encoding
defects, service closures, artificial ordering, and unresolved rights. State
what was observed, what was reconstructed, and what remains inaccessible.

### Connect the object to its research paths

Stable identifiers should connect the accessioned work to artist, project,
manifestation, provenance, rights, conservation, technical, exhibition,
bibliographic, source, and revision records. A public page should lead to the
supporting record without making the interface carry the record's full
durability burden.

### Record operational responsibility

Publish the roles, authority, method, test conditions, access permissions,
dependencies, and rights that shaped a work's presentation or preservation.
The record should identify the decision and the evidence behind it.

### Keep revisions visible

Every substantial publication should carry version, date, research cutoff,
source state, correction route, and supersession history. A stronger account
should extend the record while preserving the earlier assertion and the reason
for the change.

### Test transfer at the field level

The transferable unit is usually a field, form, workflow, source path, or
record relationship—not an institution's entire model. For each case, state
what the Museum can adopt, what requires adaptation, and which dependency keeps
the analogy bounded.
