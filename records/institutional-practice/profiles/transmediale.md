## Institutional profile

The 2012 conference *transmediale Unarchived* made the archive itself a
program subject. Its public account brings together a 25-year institutional
history, archive digitization, online dissemination, and the question of how
curators can return archival material to interpretation and circulation.
[transmediale Unarchived](https://archive.transmediale.de/content/transmediale-unarchived)

This is a useful starting point for the Museum because transmediale's archive
and program are interdependent. The archive records how a festival and its
publication forms changed as internet art, software art, online programs, and
hybrid events changed around it.

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. A long program history is made browseable by edition

The legacy archive presents annual festival history from 1988 through 2020 in
a year-and-edition structure, with special projects and related material. The
organization describes transmediale e.V. as an independent nonprofit that
maintains documentation and archives, produces print and online publications,
and runs residencies and commissions. [Archive overview](https://archive.transmediale.de/archive)
· [Organization](https://transmediale.de/de/organisation)

### 2. Publication is a continuing institutional record

The journal is an online platform for essays, conversations, and interviews,
with issue and publication-type filters. It supplies a scholarly layer around
the program, beyond event metadata. [Journal](https://archive.transmediale.de/journal/articles)

The 2016 *Across and Beyond* publication provides a second form: an edited
introduction to post-digital practices, concepts, and institutions. Its
editorial apparatus lets institutional interpretation travel with the project.
[Across and Beyond](https://archive.transmediale.de/content/across-and-beyond-post-digital-practices-concepts-and-institutions)

### 3. Program formats and original files remain part of the history

The current history records the 2021–2022 *for refusal* program, including an
Almanac updated monthly at the full moon and a microcomputer-based ceramic
portal with original files. The record therefore links event duration, online
publication, technical format, and retained source material. [History](https://transmediale.de/en/history)

## Close reading of public scholarship

transmediale's writing changes register with its subject. The archive overview
is chronological and navigational; *Unarchived* is reflective and asks what
archival work does; the journal is issue-based and discursive; the current
history tracks shifts in medium and event format. Together these forms produce
institutional memory without pretending that every program item has the same
status or completeness.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Give festivals, commissions, publications, online programs, residencies,
  archives, and artworks separate records with typed relationships.
- Preserve edition, date, format, original files, migration history, current
  access route, and later interpretation for every significant program.
- Treat publications as durable research objects with authors, contributors,
  issue/date, source files, revision history, and suggested citation.
- Keep an archive's historical cutoff, missing material, reconstructed pages,
  and current access defects visible.
- Record program material that is temporarily online as a lifecycle state with
  an explicit preservation status.

## Where the analogy ends

transmediale's archive is festival history and public scholarship. The reviewed
material does not establish title, accession, custody, condition, or
object-level conservation. The apparent 2020 endpoint of the legacy archive
and the absence of a public structured API should remain explicit. The record
should carry those gaps without inference.
