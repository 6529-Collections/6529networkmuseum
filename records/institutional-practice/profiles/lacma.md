LACMA’s [“Welcome to the Art + Tech
Lab”](https://www.lacma.org/lab) states that the Lab supports experiments in
design, creative entrepreneurship, art and industry, collaboration, and
interdisciplinary dialogue. The page links current projects to the original
1967–71 program catalogue and keeps a dated Lab Archive of artists and
projects.

The page’s dated record for Casey Reas’s *METAVASARELY* and *An Empty Room*,
alongside the artist interview [“Introducing METAVASARELY and An Empty Room,
A Two-Part Digital Work by Casey
Reas”](https://unframed.lacma.org/2023/02/13/introducing-metavasarely-and-empty-room-two-part-digital-work-casey-reas),
provides a named example of this continuity.

## *METAVASARELY* and the program archive

LACMA’s interview states that the project is a simulation and homage to Victor
Vasarely’s unrealized proposal for the original Art & Technology Program. The
proposal described a machine with lights arranged in a grid that would generate
millions of visual patterns related to Vasarely’s paintings. Reas explains that
he chose a software simulation in place of a physical electronic build.
For LACMA, he describes creating one work in each of two forms: *METAVASARELY*
and *An Empty Room*. The page says both have online and in-gallery components,
beginning with *METAVASARELY* on lacma.org.

This record is precise enough to separate the historical proposal, Reas’s
software simulation, the two-part project, and the current presentation. A
6529 program page should preserve the call, selection decision, artist brief,
source documents, project components, minting or accession event, exhibition
history, and later preservation status as linked records.

## Colloquium as a care record

Joey Heinen’s [“Examining the Life and Times of Media Art at LACMA’s First-Ever
Time-Based Media Colloquium”](https://unframed.lacma.org/node/3597) identifies
the event as **TBM IRL: Examining Lifecycles of Time-Based Media Art**, a
daylong colloquium held on October 27, 2023, at the Academy Museum of Motion
Pictures. The account names artists, gallerists, collectors, archivists,
estate representatives, and museum professionals as participants.

The article records questions about what defines authenticity across time,
who is authorized to make preservation decisions, whether artist intent is the
controlling rule, how an ideal state relates to technological change, and how
to steward works that depend on obsolescent technologies. It also identifies
artist archives, authorship and authority, AI, blockchain, and big data as
topics. The author’s stated takeaway is advocacy for the resources and labor
required to care for time-based media.

For each time-based or network-dependent work, the Museum should turn those
questions into required record fields: work definition; authorized decision
makers; acceptable change; period-specific and current environments;
accessibility and embodiment needs; dependencies; recurring labor and budget;
test evidence; and unresolved questions.

## Blockchain acquisition: ambition and evidence

LACMA’s [“LACMA Acquires Largest Collection of Blockchain
Artworks”](https://www.lacma.org/press/lacma-acquires-largest-collection-blockchain-artworks),
dated February 13, 2023, states that a gift from Cozomo de’ Medici placed 22
digital artworks by 13 artists in or promised to the permanent collection. The
release quotes LACMA’s director describing the acquisition as an impetus to
develop new standards and techniques for preserving works created on the
blockchain.

That preservation language is a stated institutional ambition. The release
documents an acquisition; it supplies no public schema or condition model. The 6529
Network Museum should publish the next layer as linked, testable documentation:
chain and token identifiers; custody and provenance events; artist, collector,
and museum rights; source and dependency inventory; display and access
requirements; condition tests; preservation decisions; and named responsible
roles.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Keep program history, artist/work records, acquisitions, exhibitions, and
  preservation builds as distinct linked events.
- Use a work-level authority and authenticity record for media works, with
  explicit rules for reinstallation, migration, and acceptable change.
- Budget recurring technical labor and record who is responsible for each
  dependency, service, and preservation test.
- Convene artists, conservators, curators, registrars, archivists, technicians,
  and rights staff around a named work and publish the resulting decisions.
- Treat blockchain preservation standards as versioned public specifications
  tested against named collection works.

## Where the analogy ends

LACMA’s Art + Tech pages combine program description, archive links, project
pages, interviews, and acquisition announcements. Those forms serve different
editorial purposes and should not be treated as one collection-data model. The
blockchain release documents the acquisition and the intention to develop
preservation standards; it does not, by itself, demonstrate a completed
technical or provenance method.
