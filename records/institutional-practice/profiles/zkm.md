ZKM was founded in 1989 by the City of Karlsruhe and the state of
Baden-Württemberg. Its planning began in 1984 and took formal shape in
“Konzept ’88.” The founding model joined a museum and media-art collection
with research institutes, studios, and a media theatre; ZKM’s current
description continues to connect research and production with exhibitions,
performances, collections, and archives. [Founding & History](https://zkm.de/en/founding-history) · [Development & Philosophy](https://zkm.de/en/development-philosophy)

ZKM reports approximately 12,000 works, 24,000 video and audio tapes, more
than 1,500 video titles available in its Media Lounge, and an audio collection
of more than 9,000 titles. These are current institutional figures, not
independent measurements. [The ZKM Collection](https://zkm.de/en/the-zkm-collection)

## Work record: *Wipe Cycle*

The official record for Frank Gillette and Ira Schneider’s *Wipe Cycle* keeps
the 1969 work, its later reconstruction, and its technical dependencies in
one public account. The record identifies a six-channel installation with
nine cathode-ray monitors, a camera, two digitized analog videos, six
Raspberry Pi 3B+ computers, Raspian, custom C/GStreamer software, a custom
video-switch board, sound, a media player, and speakers. It also links an
installation view and the 1969 schematic. [Wipe Cycle](https://zkm.de/de/werk/wipe-cycle)

The apparatus explains the work’s closed-circuit timing, so the equipment list
remains attached to the work's operation.
The record distinguishes historical documentation from the reconstruction,
but it contains a dating issue: the narrative places the reconstruction in a
2017 exhibition while image captions show both 1969/2017 and 1969/2018. That
uncertainty should remain visible until the source institution resolves it.

For the 6529 Network Museum, every migration or emulation record should retain
the original runtime, current runtime, preserved source, intervention date,
decision-maker, evidence links, and known differences between states.

## Conservation and research as operating infrastructure

ZKM describes a restoration team combining restoration, electrical engineering,
computer science, and art history. The team documents and restores works while
developing standardized documentation and long-term conservation methods.
[Restoration of Electronic and Digital Art](https://zkm.de/en/restoration-of-electronic-and-digital-art)

Its Laboratory for Antiquated Video Systems was founded in 2004. ZKM reports
that the laboratory maintains more than 300 historical video devices and can
digitize nearly fifty formats; it also describes metadata, mirroring, and the
continued value of analog holdings alongside digital copies. [Laboratory for Antiquated Video Systems](https://zkm.de/en/laboratory-for-antiquated-video-systems)

In 2017, ZKM created “Wissen – Collection, Archives & Research,” bringing
together collection fields with the institutional archive and acquired archives
of artists and theorists. [Wissen](https://zkm.de/en/wissen) ZKM also published
the 665-page *Digital Art Conservation* anthology in 2013, presenting results
of a project begun in 2010 through case studies and contributions from
theorists, restorers, programmers, and artists. [Digital Art Conservation](https://zkm.de/en/publications/digital-art-conservation)

## What the Museum should adopt

- Require a technical-constitution record for executable or networked works:
  source, dependencies, runtime, media inputs, display behavior, and capture
  conditions.
- Record original, migrated, emulated, and exhibited states separately, with
  dates, responsible reviewers, evidence, and known losses or changes.
- Route review to the competencies the work requires—art history, code or
  contract behavior, rights, infrastructure, and conservation—and retain the
  review roles with the object record.
- Publish a concise public record alongside the technical note, preservation
  history, and sources that allow another researcher to reproduce the claim.

## Where the analogy ends

ZKM’s laboratories, obsolete-equipment holdings, physical facilities, and
public funding support a material preservation program that the 6529 Network
Museum cannot reproduce at equivalent scale online. ZKM’s claims about global
size or uniqueness remain institutional self-report; the transferable evidence
is the documented record structure, conservation workflow, and explicit
handling of reconstruction states.
