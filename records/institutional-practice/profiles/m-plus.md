# M+ — Collecting Digital and Moving-Image Culture

- **Series:** A field of practice
- **Status:** public scholarship
- **Institutional author:** 6529 Network Museum
- **Version:** 1.0.0
- **Publication date:** 2026-08-04
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-08-04
- **Research apparatus:** [primary-source register](../source-register.md)

M+ is a collecting museum whose [M+ Collection](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/about-the-collection/m-collection/) includes moving image, digital installations, video games, software, and web-based works. Its public record is strongest where it keeps an artwork, an archive, a library, a conservation activity, and an access condition distinct while allowing them to be researched together.

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. Digital work is part of an interdisciplinary collection

The M+ Collection page places digital and moving-image work within a collection organized across design, visual art, architecture, and moving image. The broader [About the Collection](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/about-the-collection/) page separately identifies the M+ Collection, M+ Sigg, Library Special Collection, and Collection Archives. It also describes specialist conservators, research and analysis, treatment documentation, installation art, time-based media, and physical and digital access.

The separation matters for 6529. An archival document, a work, an installation instruction, and a public capture may be related without being interchangeable.

### 2. Collection management roles are named

The [M+ Collections Management Policy 2025](https://webmedia.mplus.org.hk/documents/collections_management_policy_2025_en.pdf) maps registration, records management, rights and reproductions, conservation, exhibition management, installation, and archives across the Collection & Exhibition Department. The policy makes the institutional route legible before a reader reaches an individual object.

### 3. Archives are research material, not decorative background

The magazine article [“An Introduction to the M+ Collection Archives”](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/an-introduction-to-the-m-collection-archives/) (22 March 2018) describes archival appraisal, preservation, cataloguing, research use, exhibitions, and Study Centre access. It treats archival fonds as material that can generate publications and exhibitions, while retaining archival description as a separate professional task.

### 4. Public access has rights boundaries

The [Research Centre Guidelines](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/research-centre/research-centre-guidelines/)
state that collection-archive and library material may require permission from
M+ and rights holders before publication. The
[Mediatheque](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/mediatheque/) provides mediated
moving-image access through playlists, filtering, and an Interactive Media
Room. It does not offer every item as an unrestricted download.

## Publication and scholarship

M+ publishes in layers: collection pages establish identity and scope; archive essays explain practice; policy pages expose institutional roles; research guidelines state access conditions; and the Mediatheque shapes encounter. The writing is operational and contextual. It does not require a single page to perform cataloguing, conservation, rights, and interpretation at once.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Create separate relationships for artwork, collection archive, library, related material, documentation, and display.
- Record rights and access at work or material level, including on-site, restricted, licensed, unavailable, and online states.
- Link technical-care notes to the exact work. A general policy supplies
  context, not work-level evidence.
- Preserve the distinction between an institution's collection, its research holdings, and its public interface.
- Provide regional-language source paths and identify the author and status of
  every translation.

## Where the analogy ends

M+'s policy demonstrates roles and intended practice; it does not establish a public conservation case for every digital work. Its scale, building, specialist staff, and rights relationships are not transferable assumptions. The Museum should adopt the record architecture and access honesty, not imply equivalent capacity.
