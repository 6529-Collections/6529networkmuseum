## Institutional profile

V2_'s [About the Archive](https://v2.nl/articles/about-the-archive/) opens by
limiting its own authority. The archive documents V2_ history, includes only
material with a direct relation to V2_, and says that completeness and
correctness are not guaranteed. It invites additions and corrections through
the editorial team. The archive includes events, people, artworks, essays,
interviews, publications, old websites, video, audio, photographs, and a
library.

That scope statement is itself a preservation record. It tells a researcher
what the archive is trying to do, what it cannot promise, and how a later person
can challenge it.

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. Migration history is part of archive description

V2_ reports that online content was migrated from an older archive in 2010
after the old software created a security problem. It identifies a DVD/disk
image route and notes that some image and movie material was excluded. Its
rights statement gives CC BY-SA as a general position while preserving
author- and photographer-specific exceptions.

### 2. Unstable-media research produces reusable structures

The 2003 *Capturing Unstable Media* project used the case studies *whisper* and
*DataCloud 2.0* to address documentation and preservation of unstable media.
Its recommendations cover documentation strategy, formal modeling, metadata,
and archival interoperability. [Capturing Unstable Media](https://v2.nl/works/capturing-unstable-media/)

The proposed structure is valuable because it treats documentation as an
interoperability problem. A future archive should be able to understand the
work, its relations, and its preservation decisions without depending on one
interface.

### 3. The archive is distributed across institutional relationships

V2_'s account of the Network for the Archiving of Design and Digital Culture,
launched 17 December 2020, describes a model that is centralized where needed
and decentralized where possible. Access, usability, resilience, and a network
of archives are treated as related design concerns. [V2_ part of Network for
the Archiving of Design and Digital Culture](https://v2.nl/blog/v2_-part-of-network-for-the-archiving-of-design-and-digital-culture)

The current catalogue supplies a public discovery surface, while V2_'s
reflection on forty years of archiving describes lost or destroyed material and
the archive as a continuing construction. [Catalogue](https://v2.nl/search/all/catalogue)
· [About 40 years of Archiving Unstable Media Art](https://v2.nl/articles/about-40-years-of-archiving-unstable-media-art/)

## Close reading of public scholarship

V2_ writes about archives through declared incompleteness. The archive page is
an editorial policy; *Capturing Unstable Media* is a research report with
methods and recommendations; the forty-year reflection is a conversation about
loss, selection, and construction. The community survey of iconic works is
useful as program and archival input, but its voting result should not be
turned into a collection-significance metric.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Give every public archive a scope statement naming inclusion rule, exclusions,
  date range, authority, correction route, and current completeness.
- Record migration trigger, old software, retained disk image or export,
  excluded material, new route, license, and known access defects.
- Use stable Museum identifiers and typed relationships among works, events,
  artists, publications, people, captures, and preservation actions.
- Publish correction and supersession paths so the archive can become more
  accurate without erasing its earlier state.
- Treat distributed storage and partner archives as named custody/access
  relationships with named custody and access roles.

## Where the analogy ends

V2_'s archive is program history and institutional memory. It does not establish
title, custody, accession, condition, or conservation completion. The current
[archive route](https://v2.nl/archive) returned HTTP 200 in a focused check but
rendered the site's page-not-found state in browser inspection on 2026-08-04;
that content-level access defect belongs in the record alongside the working
article and catalogue routes.
