# Centre Pompidou

- **Series:** A field of practice

- **Version:** 1.0.0
- **Publication date:** 2026-08-04
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-08-04

The Centre Pompidou describes a collection and research structure that joins
modern and contemporary art with film, sound, digital media, design, and
architecture. Its [organisational page](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/centre-pompidou/organisation/divisions-and-departments)
states that the National Museum of Modern Art / Industrial Design Center
inventories, conserves, restores, presents, and documents collections that
include new media. It names separate new-media and restoration departments and
identifies the Kandinsky Library as a research and documentation centre with a
reading room. These are stated institutional functions; they are not, by
themselves, proof of every conservation workflow.

The [Film and New media collection overview](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/collection/film-and-new-media)
dates this collection to 1976 and describes film, video, sound, and digital
media as emerging and ephemeral media. It states that newly acquired works are
preserved in their original format. That statement establishes institutional
intent; the *Zapping Zone* case below documents conservation research on a
named work.

The Centre's public-access system is also plural. [Pompidou+](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/pompidou)
describes more than 200,000 freely accessible digital resources across
collection records, videos, podcasts, virtual exhibitions, educational
resources, and audiovisual archives. The [research index](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/pompidou-plus/magazine/rubrique/research)
exposes language options including French, English, Spanish, Italian, Chinese,
Japanese, German, Portuguese, and Russian. Object identity is connected to
distinct research and access formats across those language routes.

## Close reading: Vera Molnar, Sans titre

The Centre's [object record for Vera Molnar's *Sans titre*](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/cxx9ErA)
identifies the work as a 1970 drawing from the cycle “A la recherche de Paul
Klee.” It records the artist, title, date, algorithmic-drawing description,
Chinese ink on paper, dimensions of 30 × 30 cm, 2002 acquisition, and inventory
number AM 2002-84. Its bibliography includes page and figure references and
links to the Bibliothèque Kandinsky catalogue.

Its structure keeps work identity, series, material description, acquisition,
inventory, and bibliography in separate fields. For a chain-native museum, the
equivalent is to keep token and
project identity, work medium, technical constitution, acquisition event, rights
status, and research references separately addressable and cross-linked.

## Conservation case: Zapping Zone

The Centre's [research and restoration page for Chris Marker's *Zapping Zone*](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/offer-to-professionals/scientific-research/zapping-zone-chris-marker)
describes a 1990 interactive installation composed of thirteen television
sets, seven Apple II GS computer stations, four luminous displays containing
eighty slides, and ten photomontages. The page documents a PATRIMA research
project with two researchers and the C2RMF, historical-documentation study,
photographic and radiographic documentation of the Apple II GS computers, and
technical service of the original equipment. It also describes planned
reinstallation and the reactivation of obsolete programs.

The page demonstrates conservation research by naming the work's components,
researchers, partner, documentary method, and technical actions. It describes
the exhibition and future public availability as planned outcomes; their
completion requires later evidence. The Museum's preservation records should
make the same temporal distinction.

The Centre's longer research writing applies the same caution to online
exhibition history. In [“Erreur 404: une histoire de l'exposition artistique
en ligne”](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/pompidou-plus/magazine/article/erreur-404-une-histoire-de-lexposition-artistique-en-ligne),
published 22 July 2025, Hugo du Plessix describes research based on eleven
interviews and more than sixteen hours of exchanges. The essay retains
funding instability, changing technical roles, obsolete tools, and criticism
of simply reproducing the white cube online. It treats institutional history
as contested infrastructure, with loss and inaccessibility left visible.

## What the Museum should adopt

- make object records resolve to bibliography, research, access, and
  preservation manifestations without merging their different purposes;
- describe digital works through components, software or hardware, duration,
  display conditions, dependencies, and technical status;
- separate completed conservation actions from planned reinstallation,
  reactivation, or public-access outcomes;
- preserve the distinction between original formats and access manifestations;
- publish the participants, methods, unresolved questions, and evidence limits
  of research projects;
- offer language and accessibility metadata for each public manifestation.

## Where the analogy ends

An object page is not a complete conservation report, and a large digital
resource count is not evidence that every resource is deeply connected to its
object. The *Zapping Zone* page documents a research and restoration project,
but its planned outputs must not be recorded as completed preservation. The
Museum should transfer the Centre's separations between identity, research,
technical evidence, and public access—not its scale or resource volume.
