MCA Chicago separates a concise collection label from the larger publication
that supplies research and interpretation. The [Collection Explorer](https://mcachicago.org/art-artists/collection/)
and a representative object page for William J. O'Brien's [*Cinaedus Table
MDCCLXXV*](https://mcachicago.org/art-artists/collection/cinaedus-table-mdcclxxv/)
use standardized fields for title, artist, date, medium, dimensions, credit,
tags, and related works. The object page is an identity and discovery record;
it does not attempt to carry a full essay or conservation dossier.

## I Was Raised on the Internet: publication as a maintained resource

MCA's [*I Was Raised on the Internet* website](https://mcachicago.org/publications/websites/i-was-raised-on-the-internet)
(2018) carries the interpretive and technical burden that the short object
record does not. It presents online-only works and readings, explains that the
works use different platforms, names production, design, editorial, curatorial,
rights, and image staff, and warns that volatile internet links may disappear.
It invites reports of missing content and identifies the site as an enduring
resource for visitors and researchers.

The exhibition page organizes approximately one hundred works from 1998 onward
into named sections and directs visitors to the exhibition website for online
works: [*I Was Raised on the Internet* exhibition](https://mcachicago.org/Exhibitions/2018/I-Was-Raised-On-The-Internet).
MCA's [websites page](https://mcachicago.org/publications/websites) describes
exhibition microsites as resources containing reading lists, playlists,
installation views, images, videos, gallery guides, reviews, and knowledge
created through research.

The decisive editorial practice is separation of genres. A label fixes identity
and discovery. A microsite gathers a research argument, works, context, credits,
rights, and public access. A book can preserve a further scholarly record. MCA's
[books catalogue](https://mcachicago.org/publications/books) includes *I Was
Raised on the Internet* among the publications that extend its exhibitions and
collection through sustained scholarship.

For the 6529 Network Museum, every public microsite or digital publication
should carry its own publication record: title, publication date, research
cutoff, scope, contributors, production system or CMS when relevant, rights,
current status, known missing content, volatile links, archive or snapshot
route, and suggested citation. The collection record should link to that
publication and leave the publication's argument intact. A correction or
missing-content report should produce a dated revision entry.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Keep object identity, interpretive essay, exhibition microsite, book, and
  technical record as distinct linked publications.
- Treat credits, rights, production roles, and source lists as part of the
  publication's evidence.
- Record link status, missing content, archive scope, current availability, and
  known differences from the original publication.
- Add a stable citation and revision history to every public web publication.
- Use short collection records to state facts, not to imply that a linked essay
  or microsite has preserved the work's runtime behavior.

## Where the analogy ends

The public MCA pages reviewed demonstrate collection-label and digital-
publication practice. They do not present a dedicated conservation dossier or
evidence of a complete digital-preservation workflow. The warning that links may
disappear is an access limitation, not proof of successful preservation. The
Museum should adopt MCA's genre separation, credits, link-status disclosure,
and correction route while keeping the limits of that evidence clear.
