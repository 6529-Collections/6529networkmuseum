## Institutional profile

LI-MA's ArtHost research starts from the practical failure of treating a net-art
URL as a preservation package. The proposed service receives documentation,
source, and domain information from an artist or collection; checks the work
and its dependencies; and gives the work a per-work virtual machine. The
architecture hosts the work while retaining the materials needed to understand
how it operates. [ArtHost research](https://li-ma.nl/article/arthost-research/)

This makes LI-MA relevant to 6529 as preservation infrastructure. Its public
site joins preservation, research, knowledge resources, projects, and
collection services; its ownership model is a separate question.

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. Preservation is a sequence of distinct operations

LI-MA's introduction to digital-art preservation distinguishes storage,
migration, digitisation, emulation, virtualization, documentation, and
reinterpretation. Migration changes source material for a new platform;
emulation preserves an earlier environment without changing the source. The
page also describes source-code storage and artist input. [What Are the Basics
of Preserving Digital Art?](https://li-ma.nl/article/preservation-introduction/)

Its storage page describes a repository for media art and an ArtHost workflow
that inventories the work, checks dependencies, transfers a domain where
needed, stores source and documentation, and provides a virtual server.
[Storage](https://li-ma.nl/article/storage/)

### 2. Documentation carries its own status

LI-MA's documentation service records content, historical context, technical
specification, configurations, installation, acquisition and exhibition
history, condition, versions, iterations, and preservation actions. It uses
text, video, screenshots, manuals, MediaWiki case studies, and GitLab version
control. [The importance of documentation in art preservation](https://li-ma.nl/article/documentation)

The Artwork Documentation Tool then draws a clear boundary around artist
intake. Its eight steps cover working notes, backups, software, hardware,
playback, wiring, key information, video registration, and additional material;
the tool states that its data is not itself stored or preserved for the future.
[Artwork Documentation Tool](https://li-ma.nl/article/artwork-documentation-tool/)

### 3. Research is fed back into collection workflows

The Collaborative Infrastructure for Digital Art project involved 15
participating collections, 10 artists, advising parties, case studies,
training, and public dissemination. LI-MA reports implementation of results in
workflow, metadata, storage, and monitoring. Its UNFOLD program treats
reinterpretation as a collaborative response to new technology, context, and
audience. [Collaborative Infrastructure for Digital Art](https://li-ma.nl/article/collaborative-infrastructure-for-digital-art/)
· [UNFOLD](https://li-ma.nl/article/unfold/)

## Close reading of public scholarship

LI-MA publishes knowledge as a set of usable forms: a preservation primer, a
case-study page, a documentation tool, a storage description, a research
report, and symposium records. The ArtHost cases name the work-specific
problem—third-party influence in *Chinese Gold*, identity and participation in
*Mouchette.org*—before describing infrastructure. This keeps technology
subordinate to the work's behavior, context, and preservation decision.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Split the Museum record into intake documentation, preservation package,
  technical constitution, monitoring, intervention history, and reinterpretation.
- At accession, collect artist instructions, source, dependencies, runtime,
  domain/service assumptions, installation behavior, and an evidence package.
- Mark documentation, storage, migration, emulation, virtualization, and
  reinterpretation as distinct actions with dates and responsible parties.
- Give each action a public summary and a restricted technical annex where
  source, rights, security, or credentials require limits.
- Use artist-facing forms as intake instruments only; route their data into a
  controlled, versioned Museum record.

## Where the analogy ends

LI-MA's institutional role is preservation and service; title-holding is
outside the evidence reviewed. Its public references to NFTs show an area in
which practice is being developed. That evidence does not establish that
every NFT has been preserved.
The Museum must also determine its own custody, rights, funding, hosting, and
long-term maintenance responsibilities. No public API was identified.
