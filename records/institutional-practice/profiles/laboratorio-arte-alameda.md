# Laboratorio Arte Alameda — Research and Documentation Center

- **Series:** A field of practice

- **Research cutoff:** 2026-08-04

Laboratorio Arte Alameda is a research, production, exhibition, and
documentation center for art and technology. The sources reviewed do not
establish a collecting-museum remit. The INAH publication [“Conservación del
acervo documental del Laboratorio Arte
Alameda”](https://revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/digitales/article/view/4637),
published 5 December 2013, begins with the practical problem of accessing,
storing, disseminating, and preserving the documentary materials generated by
institutional work. That problem gives the Center's public scholarship a
precise archival subject.

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. Institutional role is stated precisely

The official [INBA profile](https://inba.gob.mx/recinto/32) describes LAA as a space for exhibition, documentation, production, and research in practices that put art and technology into dialogue. It identifies the Príamo Lozada Documentation Center, specialized in new-media art, and lists conferences, concerts, projections, seminars, and workshops.

The opening distinction matters. LAA's public evidence establishes a research and documentation function; it should not be described as a permanent collection simply because it presents or documents digital and electronic art.

### 2. Documentary preservation is published as a case

The INAH article [“Conservación del acervo documental del Laboratorio Arte Alameda”](https://revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/digitales/article/view/4637) records the need to access, store, disseminate, and preserve materials produced through daily institutional work. Its subject is the documentary archive, not a generalized promise to preserve all digital artworks.

This is a useful model for 6529: identify the material corpus, name the risk or access problem, state the preservation decision, and keep the documentary record distinct from the artwork record.

### 3. Open repositories can have editorial control

[Modos de Oír](https://modosdeoir.inba.gob.mx/) is an open digital repository developed by LAA and Ex Teresa around sound-art practices in Mexico. Its public description identifies an editorial committee and an initial group of 120 works researched for an exhibition. The repository is therefore both an access surface and an edited research project.

### 4. Documentation is supported by a specialist center and publication

The government announcement for the [Príamo Lozada Documentation Center](https://www.gob.mx/cultura/prensa/el-laboratorio-arte-alameda-inaugurara-su-centro-de-documentacion-priamo-lozada) describes books, journals, brochures, audio, video, and other documentation made available to researchers, students, and artists. The 2025 book announcement [“Prácticas artísticas, posconceptualismo electrónico y transmodernidad”](https://inba.gob.mx/prensa/22177/laboratorio-arte-alameda-presentara-el-libro-practicas-artisticas-posconceptualismo-electronico-y-transmodernidad) demonstrates a further publication form: regional art-historical research on electronic and digital practices.

## Publication and scholarship

LAA combines institutional description, a documentation center, a formal conservation study, an editorially governed repository, and regional art-historical publication. Its writing moves from documentary problem to access and preservation decision, then outward to the history of practices. The model is valuable because it does not require documentary holdings and artworks to be the same kind of record.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Create a documentary-holdings record separate from artwork accession.
- Record the source, creator, date, format, rights, condition, and access route for exhibition and program materials.
- Use editorial review and source notes for public repositories.
- Publish conservation cases around exact materials, decisions, and unresolved limitations.
- Preserve original-language bibliographies and provide clearly identified translations.

## Where the analogy ends

The INAH conservation article concerns documentary holdings, not necessarily the conservation of digital artworks. Public evidence reviewed does not establish a complete LAA accession or digital-condition schema. The Museum should adopt the Center's documentary discipline without calling documentation custody.
