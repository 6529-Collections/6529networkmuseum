The Ars Electronica Festival began in 1979, the Prix Ars Electronica in 1987,
and the Ars Electronica Center and Futurelab in 1996. The institution now
describes its organization as an ecosystem comprising festival, award, museum,
archive, laboratory, and client-facing Solutions work. The dates are
institutional history; “ecosystem” is the institution’s own organizational
description. [About Ars Electronica](https://ars.electronica.art/about/en/) · [History – Ars Electronica Futurelab](https://ars.electronica.art/futurelab/en/about/history/) · [Organization](https://ars.electronica.art/about/en/organization/)

## Project record: Spaxels

The Futurelab’s *Spaxels* page dates the project’s main development and
performances from 2012 to 2017. It identifies the 2012 Linz commission,
explains “Spaxel” as a space-based picture element, describes centrally
controlled and choreographed drone formations, and follows the work through
later projects, Fluxels, and the swarms+art platform. [Spaxels](https://ars.electronica.art/futurelab/en/projects-spaxels/)

The page joins origin, mechanism, aesthetic rationale, collaborators, later
iterations, and related research. It documents project history and public
presentation. Title, custody, rights, and permanent collection status require
separate evidence.

## An archive with technical depth

Ars Electronica’s archive documents more than four decades of festivals,
projects, exhibitions, awards, publications, and media-art activity. Its own
account reports approximately 60,000 physical records, more than 210,000
digital entries, and 68 terabytes. Database records and files are automatically
transferred to and tested in a backup system, with additional tape backup.
Access is public where legal and technical conditions allow. [About the Ars Electronica Archive](https://ars.electronica.art/archive/en/about/)

The online archive makes Prix submissions searchable across years and
categories, documents winning projects with audiovisual and textual material,
and provides publications, photographs, videos, a Prix map, and project
histories. [Ars Electronica Archive](https://ars.electronica.art/archive/en/)

Ars Electronica also preserves earlier versions of its public web presence by
converting historical sites into static, compatible versions, repairing broken
links where possible, and adding current websites for research. These are
preservation copies with stated limits on original behavior. [Websites](https://ars.electronica.art/archive/en/websites/)

## Research made public

The Ars Electronica Center describes exhibition and laboratory spaces as a
shared research environment for artists, scientists, and visitors. Its
research page specifically identifies Deep Space 8K as a site for work on
digital avatars, virtual reality, and human–robot scenarios. [Researching the Future](https://ars.electronica.art/center/en/researching-the-future/)

The Futurelab makes selected project documentation, papers, publications,
research reports, patents, and code available through its public platform.
[Ars Electronica Futurelab](https://ars.electronica.art/futurelab/en/)
The platform therefore exposes some process artifacts alongside project
descriptions; the available materials remain project-specific and do not form a
complete technical record for every work.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Give every program event its own record: call, eligibility review, selection,
  jury decision, presentation, archive inclusion, acquisition, and accession.
  Link the records without treating one state as proof of another.
- Snapshot each public collection or program site at release, retaining the
  URL, timestamp, capture format, source revision, repaired links, and known
  losses.
- Publish preservation scripts, contract prototypes, and exhibition adapters
  with their version, inputs, outputs, assumptions, and affected works.
- Maintain redundant copies with an explicit access matrix distinguishing
  public, rights-restricted, technically unavailable, and preservation-only
  material.
- Keep awards, archive inclusion, public presentation, title, custody, rights,
  and accession as separate fields and decisions.

## Where the analogy ends

Ars Electronica combines a museum, festival, competition, laboratory,
education programs, and client work through Ars Electronica Solutions. The
6529 Network Museum should preserve authority and credit for each function when
adapting a lesson. Technological novelty, immersive scale, award status, or
prototype visibility should not be used as a substitute for collection
eligibility, provenance, rights, custody, or conservation evidence.
