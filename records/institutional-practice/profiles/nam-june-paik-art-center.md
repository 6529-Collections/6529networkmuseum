# Nam June Paik Art Center — Collection, Archive, and Media-Art Scholarship

The Nam June Paik Art Center is a collecting museum and research archive. Its [NJP Reader #13 — *Nam June Paik's Transmission: journey over two centuries*](https://njpart.ggcf.kr/books/51), published 30 April 2024, places acquisition, exhibition, documentation, preservation, authenticity, and change around a specific media-installation problem. The Center's public scholarship is most useful when the work, its source materials, and the history of their handling remain separate records.

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. Collection and archive are different institutional objects

The [Collection](https://njpart.ggcf.kr/collections) includes works by Nam June Paik, related artists, and contemporary media artists, with searchable categories for video, laser, robot, television, performance, and other media. The [Archives](https://njpart.ggcf.kr/pages/archive) separate the Nam June Paik Archive, Video Archive, Studio Archive, and Museum Archive. The structure distinguishes an artwork from the records that establish its history.

### 2. The Video Archive records carriers and source relationships

The [Video Archive](https://njpart.ggcf.kr/pages/videoarchive) records 2,285 analogue tapes acquired directly from Paik in 2003. It identifies Open-Reel, U-matic, Video 8, Betacam SP, and other formats, and distinguishes editions, installation sources, performance documentation, collaborative works, and other footage. The Center also describes videotape analysis of edits and sequences.

For 6529, this is a model for recording a work's source, edition, manifestation, documentation, and carrier without treating a single file or capture as the whole work.

### 3. Oral history becomes a publication method

The [Interview Project](https://njpart.ggcf.kr/pages/interview) records interviews with Paik's colleagues and friends and publishes monograph series and interview scripts. Oral history is not presented as an unqualified substitute for object evidence; it is a named source class that can be compared with collection, studio, and exhibition records.

### 4. Readers address specific preservation questions

NJP Reader #13 addresses outdoor media installations, acquisition, presentation, documentation, preservation, and the relation between authenticity and change. Earlier, [NJP Reader #1 — *Contributions to an Artistic Anthropology*](https://njpart.ggcf.kr/books/31), published 18 December 2009, positioned a reader as a scholarly extension of exhibition and research rather than as a general institutional statement.

## Publication and scholarship

The Center combines finding aids, format inventories, oral histories, exhibition histories, and authored scholarly readers. Each form carries a different evidentiary task. The result is a publication system in which technical media analysis can sit beside historical and theoretical writing without losing the identity of the source material.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Separate artwork, edition, source, installation, performance, documentation, and carrier records.
- Record format, physical support, source relationship, and digitization or analysis event.
- Treat artist and collaborator interviews as identified evidence with date and role.
- Publish conservation essays around exact works and decisions about authenticity, change, and reconstruction.
- State whether research access is on site, appointment-only, digitally available, restricted, or temporarily unavailable.

## Where the analogy ends

The Center's archive is materially specific to Paik's estate, studio, and analogue media. It cannot be mapped directly onto token, contract, custody, or wallet facts. Archive presence and research access do not establish title or accession.
