## Institutional profile

Serpentine Arts Technologies is presented by Serpentine Galleries as a
programme combining artist commissions, creative R&D, and strategy. Its
official [Blockchain programme page](https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/arts-technologies/blockchain/)
places the Legal Lab within that programme and describes the lab as an online
research project, running from September 2019 onward, on how law can support
collaborations across art, science, and technology. Serpentine's [Future Art
Ecosystems page](https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/future-art-ecosystems/)
describes FAE as an annual strategic briefing. These pages establish a
programme of commissioning, inquiry, and public strategy inside Serpentine
Galleries; they do not establish a separate museum or a collection-preservation
operation.

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. A commission can be a live, browser-based work with a fully named production record

The work page for Danielle Brathwaite-Shirley's [*I DIDNT REALISE YOU THOUGHT
LIKE THAT*](https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/i-didnt-realise-you-thought-like-that/)
dates the project from 21 May 2026 onward and specifies web and mobile access
with subtitles. It describes a game built from the fictional world of *Below
the Blue Line*: negative online comments become events in a post-apocalyptic
world, and a real-time feed presents factions and competing accounts of truth.
The player decides whom or what to let through a door; those choices produce
different viewpoints and endings. The page names Serpentine Arts Technologies
as commissioner and producer, Danielle Brathwaite-Shirley as lead artist and
concept creator, Beyond Code Collective as a collaborator, Fonz Morris as
developer, and the curatorial and production team.

The public record demonstrates network-native commissioning through the work's
medium, access surfaces, interaction, credits, and current status. Preservation
status is not stated.

### 2. FAE3 turns infrastructure research into a dated, downloadable artifact

The [FAE3 launch page](https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/future-art-ecosystems-3-live-art-x-decentralised-tech-launch/)
dates the online release to 25 November 2022 and calls FAE3 an annual
strategic briefing. It describes interviews with specialists in art,
decentralised technology, innovation policy, and civic practice; prospective
strategies for existing and new cultural organizations; and a participatory
Pol.is workshop asking how to build a more interoperable cultural sector.

The exact report, [*Future Art Ecosystems: Art x Decentralised Tech
(FAE3)*](https://futureartecosystems.org/briefing/fae3/), is a co-written and
edited publication with named contributors, advisors, researchers, designers,
and an associated artwork. Its opening pages place the report after the
decentralised-technology hype cycle and set out a “long(er) view” of cultural
infrastructure. The report maps legacy institutional systems against
decentralised proposals, then develops its argument through interviews and
operational questions. The result is public research with a method and an
edition. Its prospective strategies remain recommendations; adoption requires
separate evidence.

### 3. Legal inquiry is attached to concrete production and transaction problems

The Legal Lab's exact paper, [*The Legal Ambiguities of Art Collaborations and
their Compatibility with NFTs*](https://d37zoqglehb9o7.cloudfront.net/uploads/2020/05/Serpentine-Legal-Lab-Paper_Art-Collaborations-NFTs-FINAL-copy.pdf),
is by Alana Kushnir and dated 27 May 2021. It examines how NFT-platform terms
handle collaborative authorship during minting, sale, and resale. The paper
tests the problem against platform terms, distinguishes a singular-author
assumption from the actual labor of artists and technologists, and follows the
consequences through payment distribution, resale royalties, moral rights, and
crediting. It ends by asking collaborators to establish rights expectations and
platforms to make their terms more compatible with collaborative practice; it
also states that the paper is not legal advice.

The practice translates a production problem into a public document that identifies the
relevant terms, affected collaborators, unresolved ambiguity, and possible
design response.

### 4. One inquiry is published through several public surfaces

Serpentine's materials give an inquiry different forms without treating them
as interchangeable. *I DIDNT REALISE YOU THOUGHT LIKE THAT* is a playable web
and mobile commission with an access statement and production credits. FAE3
has a launch event, a downloadable report, a public workshop, and a separate
artifact site. Legal Lab has a programme page and a direct PDF research paper.
Together these surfaces give the work, research argument, event, and credit
record their own public routes.

## Close reading of public scholarship

The *I DIDNT REALISE YOU THOUGHT LIKE THAT* page begins with what the player
does and what the system does in response. Its writing moves from premise to
mechanic: the graphic-novel world, the simulated feed, the factions, the door,
and the consequences of choosing who enters. The page then shifts into a
production register that names the commission, producer, curator, artist,
developer, and collaborator. That sequence keeps the work legible before it
explains the institution around it. It also avoids a generic claim that
technology “changes society”; it identifies a feed, a gate, a choice, and a
different ending. The limitation is material and consequential: the page
documents a live online work as ongoing, not a preservation test or a stable
historical snapshot.

The Legal Lab paper uses a more formal research voice. Its headings follow the
transactional sequence—collaborative authorship and minting, first sale,
resale, royalties, conditions of sale, moral rights, and crediting. It moves
between the wording of platform terms and the practical situation of a
collaborating artist and technologist. It shows how a sole-author warranty,
automated payment rule, or broad platform licence
fails to describe the work's actual production. Its date matters: the reviewed
terms and platform list belong to May 2021, and its legal analysis cannot be
treated as current legal advice without jurisdiction-specific review.

FAE3 uses a different register again: a named, co-authored briefing that
combines systems diagrams, interviews, vocabulary, historical framing, and
prospective recommendations. Its writing openly moves between diagnosis and
speculation. That makes the document useful as an institutional-thinking
artifact, but it also marks the boundary between a published ambition and a
demonstrated operational change.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Commission network-native works with records that name the artist, developer, curator, producer, collaborators, access surface, device constraints, current status, and external dependencies.
- Publish infrastructure research as a dated artifact with named authors, contributors, method, source terms, and a clear label for recommendations that have not become policy.
- Make collaborative authorship and rights part of the acquisition and commissioning record: identify contributors, permissions, display and distribution rights, credit expectations, resale or revenue rules, and the governing jurisdiction.
- Give each inquiry the public form it needs: playable work, concise access record, longer essay or report, event documentation, downloadable artifact, and machine-readable identity where applicable.
- For chain-native works, test one exact commission through the whole lifecycle—commission, contract, mint or deployment, access, transfer, attribution, rights change, and migration—before generalizing the model.

## Where the analogy ends

- Serpentine's cited pages demonstrate commissioning, research, legal inquiry, and strategy publication. They do not demonstrate an accession workflow, chain custody, condition reporting, or long-term preservation. Do not import a preservation claim into this profile.
- FAE3 explicitly formulates prospective strategies. Its discussion of public cultural infrastructure is an argument and a design horizon, not evidence that its proposed interoperability or governance arrangements exist in the Museum.
- A playable page marked “ongoing” proves a current access route at the research date. It does not prove fixity, reproducibility across devices, source-code retention, dependency control, or future access.
- The Legal Lab paper's platform terms, examples, and legal analysis are dated 2021 and jurisdiction-sensitive. They are research inputs for a rights review, not a substitute for legal advice or an adopted Museum policy.
- A commission-led R&D programme has different obligations from a permanent collection. A chain-native museum must add title, custody, rights, preservation, correction, and accession gates after the experimental phase.
