Walker Art Center's interdisciplinary collecting practice treats a live work as
a chain of commission, presentation, documentation, acquisition, training, and
future presentation. The [Interdisciplinary Initiative](https://www.walkerart.org/interdisciplinary-initiative/)
describes this chain through named projects. Maria Hassabi's *STAGING: solo*
was acquired in collaboration with the artist with a training plan, while the
page distinguishes archival and sculptural iterations from the live work. Jason
Moran's multipart project links commission, exhibition, catalogue, concerts,
digital publication, acquisition, and future stage guidelines.

## Collecting performance without collapsing ownership and documentation

Susannah Schouweiler's ["Collecting Performance"](https://www.walkerart.org/reader/collecting-performance/)
(December 15, 2011) records a Walker workshop involving scholars, writers,
developers, performers, presenters, archivists, curators, and videographers.
Its questions are concrete: what is collected and tagged; who controls
documentation; how do ownership, authorship, and profit-sharing differ; and how
can an archive make the traces of performance encounterable to a public.

The essay reports a distinction that matters for 6529: a commissioning or
collecting institution may hold documentation or a public commitment without
owning the artist's entire performance practice. Walker's language of a
"living archive" describes traces, recordings, notes, scores, rehearsals,
sets, costumes, and audience responses as related evidence, not as identical
substitutes for the event.

The [Walker Collection](https://www.walkerart.org/art-artists/visual-arts/walker-collection/)
page adds an acquisition sequence: strategic planning, board approval,
accession, and an artist questionnaire issued by registrars. It describes a
Long-Range Acquisitions Plan developed through extended institutional dialogue.
The page demonstrates a process claim; it does not establish that every public
object page exposes the full process.

## Living Collections Catalogue and publication genre

Walker publishes interdisciplinary research through essays, commissions,
videos, and the [Living Collections Catalogue](https://www.walkerart.org/interdisciplinary-initiative/).
The writing is project-sequential: it identifies the commission, the public
presentation, the acquired manifestation, the training or documentation needed
to repeat it, and the future conditions under which it may be shown. Walker's
[publishing page](https://www.walkerart.org/walker-publishing/print-publications/)
also makes editors, designers, contributors, object entries, essays, and artist
collaboration visible as part of the publication record.

For the 6529 Network Museum, a live or performative work should have a
manifestation ledger with separate entries for commission, event, installation,
archive, acquired rights, artist instructions, training, and future guidelines.
The Museum should record ownership, documentation rights, display authority,
and acceptable change as different assertions. An artist questionnaire should
be versioned, attributed, and linked to the accession or commission it informs.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Model live works as linked manifestations. A stable work record connects the
  events without flattening them into one object state.
- Preserve the distinction between ownership, documentation, archive, and
  public presentation.
- Record artist instructions, training, future-stage guidance, and the authority
  for changes to each manifestation.
- Link commission, exhibition, publication, acquisition, and archive events by
  date and stable identifier.
- Credit editors, designers, artists, researchers, and technical contributors
  in the publication record.

## Where the analogy ends

Walker’s strongest evidence concerns performance, interdisciplinary commissions,
and an institutional discussion published in 2011. The model depends on
performance-specific labor, artist relationships, board process, and a large
library and archive. It is not public evidence of a general digital-conservation
workflow. The Museum should transfer the manifestation and authority model,
not claim equivalent staffing or preservation capacity.
