The V&A’s public conservation writing documents a digital-care program being
built through casework, equipment, workflows, and collaboration. Anna
Mladentseva’s [“What does conserving the digital look
like?”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/projects/what-does-conserving-the-digital-look-like)
describes the museum’s first dedicated digital conservation studio and the
work required to establish disk imaging, write-blocking, and related
procedures.

## *Shaping Form 14/5/2007*: a documented intervention

Mladentseva describes Ernest Edmonds’s *Shaping Form 14/5/2007* as a MacMini
connected to a liquid-crystal display monitor, with a USB camera embedded in
the monitor frame. The software, programmed primarily in Max/MSP, responds to
movement by generating vertical bands of varying thickness and colour.

For this work, the article reports that the conservator disassembled the
artist-provided MacMini, changed an internal battery, extracted the hard drive,
and made a bit-for-bit disk image with a forensic imager and write-blocker.
The image preserves file and directory structures, the operating system, and
deleted files, and can support later migration or emulation. Legacy adapters
were required to read the drive, and dust removal was completed during
reassembly.

The work was then run with original artist-provided parts and assessed in both
dormant and activated states. The conservator recorded Max/MSP console errors
and assessed whether they affected the work. The article reports treatment
proposals and a preservation plan, analysis of Max/MSP, Jitter, and Java source
code, and recovery of multiple versions of software-architecture documents
from deleted files. It presents those materials as evidence for understanding
the program’s functionality, artistic intention, and making process.

The 6529 Network Museum should publish case studies with the same minimum
structure: object definition, environment, intervention trigger, tools and
risks, fixity evidence, condition observations, source and dependency findings,
artist or estate input, decision authority, and follow-up preservation plan.

## Collection access around real use

Kati Price’s [“Redesigning the V&A’s collections
online”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/digital/redesigning-the-vas-collections-online)
states that V&A collections information was distributed across three public
locations and seven systems. It identifies object pages with no onward paths
as “digital culs-de-sac” and describes a redesign intended to connect objects
with makers, themes, stories, research, and making.

For 6529, an object page should expose links to the project, artist, chain and
provenance events, technical dependencies, related works, conservation builds,
and correction or amendment history. A catalogue review should test actual
questions: can a visitor move from token to artist and project; can a researcher
compare media, dependencies, or provenance events; and can a contributor locate
the governing source and propose a correction?

## What the Museum should adopt

- Record original environment, active and dormant condition, intervention
  trigger, tools, fixity results, errors, dependencies, source versions, and
  rights in every digital-work record.
- Require a preservation plan when an object depends on removable media,
  obsolete hardware, proprietary software, external services, or artist-made
  code.
- Link each intervention to an evidence package containing images, logs,
  version identifiers, treatment decisions, and artist or estate responses.
- Separate the stable object record from dated practitioner essays, then link
  both directions.
- Test collection navigation with visitor and research tasks before treating a
  catalogue redesign as complete.

## Where the analogy ends

The V&A’s *Shaping Form* account is one documented case, and the article says
source-code access depended on support from the artist. Its public writing also
describes digital-care workflows as newly established. A decentralized museum
should not assume that source, hardware, or third-party service access will be
available; it should record those absences, rights limits, and unresolved
dependencies at accession.
