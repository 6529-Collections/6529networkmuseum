## Institutional profile

A current page for knowbotic research's *minds of concern, New York version*
(2002) begins with a preservation fact: Flash no longer renders in current
browsers. HEK supplies a browser-emulated route for the software context of
2002, Internet Archive links for external material, and a separate
non-emulated mobile route. Popups and external links are listed as limitations.
The record therefore describes access as a dated technical condition, with no
guarantee that the route will remain permanent. [*minds of concern*](https://moc.hek.ch/)

HEK's collection page places born-digital, software, net-based, interactive,
and participatory works at the center of its remit. It describes acquisitions
from a 2010–2012 research project, donations, and permanent loans, alongside
care for digital works in the Swiss Federal Art Collection and Kunstkredit.
[Collection](https://hek.ch/en/collection/)

## Demonstrated practices

### 1. Collection work starts with technical inquiry

HEK says inventory begins with technical staff and external IT or conservation
expertise. Documentation is followed by a preservation solution matched to the
work. Its public research pages link case studies on emulation, digital
archiving, and specific works. [Research and
Restauration](https://hek.ch/en/collection/research-and-restauration/)

The digital-archive study reports a collection of more than 90 works and about
150 digital archival information packages. It describes automated integrity
checks and replacement of corrupted material from correct copies. Those are
institution-reported figures and procedures; the transferable evidence is the
record structure and the explicit fixity problem. [From a manual to an
automated digital archive at HEK](https://hek.ch/en/collection/research-and-restauration/fallstudie-digitales-archiv-hek/)

### 2. Conservation reports retain the work's context

The *TraceNoizer* case study follows a web-based work made in 2001 through its
offline and online history, HEK's 2017 acquisition, and the technical and
techno-cultural context needed for presentation. It names artist interview,
web documentation, and the supplied digital archive as evidence. [Conservation
case study: TraceNoizer by LAN](https://hek.ch/en/collection/research-and-restauration/conservation-case-study-tracenoizer-by-lan/)

The *Communimage* report records a 2020 version acquired in 2023, earlier
artist updates, a 2024 local demonstration, source code stored outside the
website container, and Java/JVM dependency. Its account joins interaction,
source, installation problem, and proposed presentation state. [Communimage
case study](https://cms.hek.ch/files/downloads/CaseStudy-Communimage.pdf)

### 3. Collection records can describe generative chain-native work

HEK's *ConcretionX144X160 #3* page identifies three acquired works from a
114-part generative NFT series, the work's Game Boy-like format, four-color
palette, animation, online/NFT form, fxhash link, and the three collection
iterations. The page supplies a concrete object record while keeping the token
page from standing in for the institutional record. [*ConcretionX144X160
#3*](https://hek.ch/en/collection/artworks/concretionx144x160-3/)

HEK also documents public software and network programs through [Projects](https://hek.ch/en/projects/),
including HEK Net Works from March 2020 and Net Encounters from January to
June 2021.

## Close reading of public scholarship

HEK's case-study prose moves in a useful order: identify the work; establish
its historical and acquisition context; describe behavior and dependencies;
name the intervention; then leave residual uncertainty visible. The writing
keeps “digital preservation” concrete. Java, Flash, source location, external
links, browser behavior, and local demonstration are the terms in which the
argument is made.

## What the Museum should adopt

- Require a work-specific technical-constitution record covering source,
  dependencies, runtime, browser, external services, hardware, chain/token,
  mutability, and display assumptions.
- Record original, current, migrated, emulated, documentary, and fallback
  states separately, with dates, evidence, responsible reviewers, and known
  losses.
- Publish conservation case studies that connect acquisition, artist input,
  technical diagnosis, intervention, current access, and remaining limits.
- Add catalogue fields for public data route, rights status, and whether the
  Museum holds source, capture, runtime, token, or only permission to link.

## Where the analogy ends

HEK has collection authority, conservation staff, and technical partnerships
that the 6529 Network Museum does not automatically possess. An NFT catalogue
entry does not establish title, custody, rights clearance, or preservation
completion. No public structured API was identified in the reviewed material.
