# Amazônia: photographs, books and a digital edition


*Amazônia* grew from six years of Sebastião Salgado's work in the Brazilian
Amazon. Presented as a book and exhibition in 2021, the project brought
forest, rivers, mountains and Indigenous communities together in a large
body of black-and-white photographs. Lélia Wanick Salgado designed the book
and curated the exhibition.[^1]

## Making and presenting the photographs

The work required repeated expeditions and substantial support. Salgado's
2022 account describes travelling with guides, a translator, a cook and
an assistant, carrying food and equipment by boat. His photographic
equipment included digital cameras and a large portable studio backdrop.[^2]

The project reached audiences through carefully constructed presentations.
At MAXXI, the exhibition ran from 1 October 2021 to 21 August 2022.
Its sections addressed aerial views, water, rain and mountain terrain.[^3]
At the Science Museum, large photographs hung from the ceiling alongside
structures evoking Indigenous homes. Jean-Michel Jarre's commissioned
soundtrack accompanied the exhibition, and films presented Indigenous
leaders speaking about the future of their territories.[^4]

These arrangements made the photographs part of an encounter that included
movement, sound and spoken testimony. Lélia's design brought the individual photographs into this larger presentation.

## The 2022 digital edition

Sotheby's announced a 5,000-work *Amazônia* photographic NFT edition,
with a blind mint scheduled for 12 October 2022.[^5] The Museum's six
works come from this edition. Each has an Ethereum token identifier,
an associated metadata record and a JPEG image. The metadata supplies
location, category and date fields used in the Museum's object entries.

The six photographs were made between 2016 and 2019. Their acquisition as *Amazônia: Forest, Water, and Community* gives these
works a permanent home in the Museum and makes them available to view
together. The collection essay discusses the selection; the individual
entries identify each photograph.[^6]

## The Museum's edition records

The Museum retains the image files, issuer metadata and provenance records
for all six tokens. These establish the particular digital objects acquired
and support their continued care. The source photographs include five
horizontal images and one vertical portrait.

The [technical record](technical-and-condition-review.md) documents
source files and display preparation.[^7]
