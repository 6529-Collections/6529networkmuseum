# Sebastião Salgado: a practice of sustained projects


Sebastião Salgado built his career around photographic projects pursued over
years and assembled into books and exhibitions. *Workers*, *Migrations*,
*Genesis* and *Amazônia* address subjects on a large scale: manual labour,
displacement, the natural world and the Amazon. Their cumulative form is
central to his practice. Individual photographs acquire further meaning
through the places, people and events brought alongside them.

## Working in series

Born in Aimorés, Brazil, in 1944, Salgado trained in economics and worked for
the International Coffee Organization before turning to photography in 1973.
He worked with Sygma, Gamma and Magnum Photos. In 1994 he and Lélia Wanick
Salgado established Amazonas Images.[^1]

His economic training preceded a sustained interest in how people work and
live within larger social conditions. *Other Americas* and *Sahel*, both
published in 1986, were followed by *Workers* in 1993 and *Migrations* in 2000.
Writing in 1999, Lisa Hostetler identified the series as essential to the
context Salgado gives his subjects.[^2]

This way of working made editing and publication consequential parts of the
practice. Lélia shaped the design of the books and the arrangement of the
exhibitions. Her work gave the accumulated photographs an order, pace and physical
setting.[^3]

## Documentary photography and beauty

Salgado's dramatic black-and-white style has prompted a substantial critical
debate. Discussing *Migrations* in 2002, Susan Sontag questioned its treatment
of displacement as a global subject and its use of unnamed subjects. She
argued that these choices weakened the photographs' account of particular
histories.[^4]

Chris Wiley, writing in 2025, defended the force of Salgado's style. In his
reading, photographic beauty could intensify the act of bearing witness.[^5]

## The environmental projects

*Genesis*, published in 2013, brought landscapes, wildlife and Indigenous
communities to the centre of his work. *Amazônia*, published in 2021,
concentrated on the Brazilian Amazon. These projects developed alongside Sebastião and
Lélia's work at Instituto Terra, which they founded in 1998 to restore
forest in Brazil's Atlantic Forest region.[^1]

Salgado died in Paris on 23 May 2025. The Museum holds six works from
the digital edition issued during his lifetime in 2022.[^1][^6]
