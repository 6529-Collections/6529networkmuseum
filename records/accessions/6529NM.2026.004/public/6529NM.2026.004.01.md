# Forest, Jaú River, Jaú National Park, Amazonas

**Artist:** Sebastião Salgado (1944–2025)  
**Date:** 2019  
**Project:** Amazônia  
**Medium:** Black-and-white digital photograph; JPEG associated with an ERC-721 token  
**Source image:** 3543 × 2362 pixels  
**Edition identifier:** Token 226  
**Museum identifier:** 6529NM.2026.004.01, allocated for accession processing

## Label

Slender pale trunks rise through a dense mass of vegetation. Palm fronds
spread and droop above them; smaller leaves gather around their bases.
The trunks provide strong vertical lines, while the fronds divide into
many finer marks. Dark gaps between plants give the forest depth without
opening a clear route through it.

The issuer places the photograph beside the Jaú River in Jaú National Park,
Amazonas. Salgado brings the vegetation close enough for individual forms
to remain distinct, even as they overlap across the frame. Within the
Museum's six-work group, this view gives the forest a different scale
from the aerial photographs. The continuous canopy seen from above becomes
a crowded arrangement of trunks, leaves and shadow.[^1]

## Trunks, fronds and intervals

The clearest verticals gather near the centre and to its left. Their pale
surfaces separate them from the vegetation behind, allowing several trunks
to be followed through much of the photograph's height. They form a loose
group within the broader image. Differences in spacing and inclination
keep their repetition irregular.

Above them, the palm fronds establish another rhythm. Long stems bend
outward, carrying closely spaced leaflets that fall along their sides.
Some fronds are brightly lit and sharply separated from the shade. Others
overlap until their individual outlines become difficult to follow. The
eye moves between the large arcs of the leaves and their much smaller
divisions.

The lower field has a different density. Smaller plants and crossing stems
obscure the bases of the taller growth. The view does not open onto an
unoccupied forest floor. Vegetation continues down to the edge, leaving the
viewer to infer depth from overlap and from the darker intervals between
plants. The apparent wall of growth contains several planes, but none
offers an unobstructed passage into the distance.

Salgado's framing sustains that condition. There is no open sky or horizon,
and plants extend beyond every side. A branch or frond can lead attention
toward an edge without bringing the view to a conclusion. The photograph
describes a portion of the forest whose growth continues outside it.

Black and white gives the pale trunks particular prominence. Their broad
vertical marks can be recognized at a small scale, while the smaller
leaves merge more readily into texture. Enlargement changes the balance:
individual fronds and the fine interruptions around each trunk become
easier to distinguish. The picture rewards movement between an overall
reading and the examination of smaller areas.

The aerial river photograph presents forest as a broad canopy surrounding
a light channel. Here, the channel is absent from the visible composition;
the Jaú River belongs to the geographical caption.[^1] This difference is
central to the pairing. The elevated view reveals continuity across a large
area, while the closer view reveals the density and overlap within that
continuity. Neither scale supplies the information of the other.

The source category, `FOREST`, identifies the broad subject. Salgado's
photograph gives it a particular structure: repeated uprights, long curved
fronds, small leaves and deep intervals of shade. The relation between
these forms holds the image together while keeping the vegetation varied.
