# Nova Esperança community, Rio Gregório Yawanawá Indigenous Territory, Acre

**Artist:** Sebastião Salgado (1944–2025)  
**Date:** 2016  
**Project:** Amazônia

**Source image:** 3543 × 2362 pixels

## Label

A figure wearing a broad feather headdress occupies the right foreground.
An extended arm and a long upright object draw attention across the body,
while several people stand farther back in the clearing. Feathers, branches
and palm fronds meet above them, creating a dense upper field against which
the figures remain distinct.

The source caption places the photograph at Nova Esperança, a Yawanawá
community in Acre, in 2016. Its accompanying account describes the renewal
of language and cultural practices after sustained pressure from rubber
plantation owners and missionaries. The picture belongs to that account,
but it also records a particular arrangement of people and gestures.
Foreground presence and the more distant gathering give the scene two
different centres of attention.[^1]

## A figure and a gathering

The foreground figure is close enough for the headdress to occupy a large
part of the photograph. Feathers spread outward from the head in a broad
arc, extending the figure's presence beyond the width of the body. The
upright object beside the arm introduces another strong direction. Its dark
surface and shaped edges separate it from the brighter intervals behind it.

This close view does not isolate the figure. A clearing opens on the left,
where other people appear at a smaller scale. Their spacing makes the
depth of the scene legible. The foreground arm reaches across the picture
without closing that space, allowing attention to pass between the nearby
body and the people beyond. The relationship is different from the Marubo
portrait's concentrated encounter against a plain background: here,
surrounding activity remains part of the photograph's structure.

The vegetation complicates the separation. Branches run above the gathering,
and long fronds overlap the radiating feathers. These forms share directions
and narrow divisions of light, but they retain different attachments. The
headdress belongs to a person; the branches connect to the place around the
gathering. Salgado makes them visually contiguous without making their
functions interchangeable.

The lower part of the photograph is comparatively open. Bodies and objects
can be followed more readily against the clearing than against the overhead
foliage. The shift between open ground and dense vegetation produces two
speeds of looking: the figures can be located quickly, while the intersections
above them take longer to separate. The foreground figure participates in
both areas, joining the scene's more legible and more crowded surfaces.

The caption identifies Nova Esperança and the Rio Gregório Yawanawá
Indigenous Territory. The historical account attached to it emphasizes
cultural renewal, especially language and the transmission of knowledge
between generations.[^1] A separate Museu do Índio account, adapted from
Camargo-Tavares's 2013 research, describes the development of distinct
Yawanawá organizations at Nova Esperança and Mutum, and a festival at
Nova Esperança begun in 2002.[^2] That history gives cultural activity an
institutional and political setting. It belongs to people organizing their
own public life, with relationships extending beyond the clearing.

The photograph gives this history a particular scale. The aerial works
spread forest and water across immense distances; this frame brings a
person close while retaining the presence of others. Its contribution to
the six-work group is the relation between those distances within one
scene. The foreground figure commands attention, but the gathering
continues behind him.
