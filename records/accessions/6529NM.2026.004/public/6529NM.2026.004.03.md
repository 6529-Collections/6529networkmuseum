# Aerial view, Roraima: clouds and rain

**Artist:** Sebastião Salgado (1944–2025)  
**Date:** 2018  
**Project:** Amazônia  
**Medium:** Black-and-white digital photograph; JPEG associated with an ERC-721 token  
**Source image:** 3543 × 2362 pixels  
**Edition identifier:** Token 2059  
**Museum identifier:** 6529NM.2026.004.03, allocated for accession processing  
**Credit:** Gift of punk6529. Photograph © Sebastião Salgado.  
**Text:** 6529 Network Museum, version 0.1.1, 13 September 2026; review pending

## Label

Clouds rise above a forest broken into areas of sunlight and shade. Near the
horizon, rain falls in pale curtains. The photograph joins these separate
heights through their visible relations: clouds overhead, rainfall in the
distance and broad shadows on the ground. A bright river segment on the left
briefly interrupts the forest's fine texture.

The source caption places the view in Roraima in 2018. Compared with the
Museum's other aerial photograph from the same state, this image gives the
sky a much larger role. Its subject is the changing relation between land and
weather, made legible by a moment of uneven illumination.[^1]

## A view distributed across sky and land

The largest cloud rises to the right of centre, but the photograph has several
competing points of attention. Bright cloud tops extend laterally across the
sky. Far below them, illuminated stretches of forest alternate with dark
areas. Between these two fields lies a narrow, less distinct distance through
which the rain falls. The eye moves among these levels rather than settling
on one central object.

The sky is notably dark above the brightest clouds. This contrast gives their
upper surfaces a defined shape, while the undersides settle into broad bands
of grey. Smaller clouds diminish toward the horizon. Differences in size and
overlap make the sky occupy depth, much as changes in tree texture establish
distance in the landscape below. The clouds constitute a substantial part of
the photographed space.

Rain provides a more delicate connection between the two. Pale vertical
curtains appear beneath distant clouds, partly veiling the land. Their edges
are less distinct than those of the nearer cloud masses. Salgado records a
transition in visibility: the far landscape remains present, but atmospheric
conditions change how much of it can be seen.

The shadows on the forest give weather another visible form. A shaded ridge
can appear heavier and nearer than the brightly lit ground beyond it. Across
the frame, the disposition of light modifies the apparent shape of the land.
The photograph therefore describes both terrain and an arrangement that will
change as the clouds move. Its depth is partly a product of that temporary
illumination.

An aerial view offers breadth, but breadth can also make detail difficult to
interpret. From this distance, the tree canopy becomes a finely worked
surface. Individual trees are most distinguishable near the bottom of the
frame; farther away, they merge. The photograph lets the viewer relate a large
expanse of forest to the sky without providing an account of particular
places within it.

The edition metadata identifies Roraima and uses the broad category `AERIAL
VIEWS`.[^1] It gives no river name for the bright segment on the left. Nor
does the image establish a climate trend or quantify a rainfall event. Its
contribution is more specific: it makes the occurrence of rain and the
distribution of light observable within one extensive view. Environmental
interpretation begins from that visible relation and needs further evidence
for claims at other scales.

Together with token 2768, the photograph gives the Museum two different aerial
structures. In the river view, successive bends offer a continuous line to
follow. Here, the eye moves between dispersed clouds, rain curtains and areas
of light on the forest. The shared state and metadata category make the
difference especially useful. A classification that groups the two images
together leaves their most consequential formal distinction unexplained.
