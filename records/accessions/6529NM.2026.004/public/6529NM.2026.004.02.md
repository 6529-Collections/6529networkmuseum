# Imeri mountain range, Amazonas

**Artist:** Sebastião Salgado (1944–2025)  
**Date:** 2018  
**Project:** Amazônia  
**Medium:** Black-and-white digital photograph; JPEG associated with an ERC-721 token  
**Source image:** 3543 × 2362 pixels  
**Edition identifier:** Token 1117  
**Museum identifier:** 6529NM.2026.004.02, allocated for accession processing  
**Credit:** Gift of punk6529. Photograph © Sebastião Salgado.  
**Text:** 6529 Network Museum, version 0.1.1, 13 September 2026; review pending

## Label

A dark ridge rises across the foreground. Behind it, a long cliff presents
an almost level upper edge; to the left, forest extends into a pale distance.
The overlap of these forms establishes depth without a building or figure
to measure it. Light separates the partly illuminated rock face from the
nearer ridge's shadow.

The source caption identifies the Imeri mountain range in Amazonas. Within
Salgado's *Amazônia*, this photograph gives the forest a sharply interrupted
terrain. The mountain divides a view that might otherwise appear continuous,
and its diagonal foreground makes the distant plain seem to open farther
away.[^1]

## Overlapping ground

Two edges organize the mountain photograph. The near ridge crosses the frame
diagonally, rising steeply toward the right. Farther back, the cliff's upper
edge extends across the image with much less variation in height. Their
intersection gives the photograph its principal spatial relation. The ridge
obscures part of the farther formation while directing the eye toward it.

The difference between the two masses also depends on light. The near ridge
is largely shaded. Its surface remains visible, but dark tones join it into
a relatively continuous form. On the farther cliff, patches of illumination
reveal changes in the face of the rock. Those lighter areas establish a
second plane. The photograph would lose much of its depth if these surfaces
were rendered at the same brightness.

The forest floor extends leftward beneath the cliff and toward the distance.
Near the lower edge, separate crowns give it a coarse texture. Farther away,
that texture becomes finer until it merges with haze. This progression is
one of the photograph's measures of scale. It allows the viewer to infer
considerable distance without identifying a single tree or knowing the
height of the formation.

Clouds complicate the mountain's silhouette. Several bright forms gather above
the plateau and toward the upper right. Their uneven contours sit against
the comparatively restrained line of the cliff. The clouds register a
temporary condition around a formation whose visual weight suggests a longer
duration. Salgado brings both into one arrangement of light and edges.

The caption identifies the range, state and year. It does not name an
individual peak.[^1] The near-horizontal cliff top may prompt comparisons with
other mountain photographs in *Amazônia*, but resemblance supplies no basis
for replacing the caption with a more familiar place name. The particular
identity retained here rests on the issuer's record.

The mountain view also affects the reading of the Museum's river photograph,
token 2768. There, pale water supplies a continuous line through the woodland.
Here, the dark ridge interrupts continuity and blocks part of the farther
view. Placed together, the images make the Amazon's terrain difficult to
reduce to a single expansive surface. One photograph leads the eye through
the landscape; the other gives substantial attention to an obstruction
within it.

At a large display size, the shaded ridge retains detail that a small
thumbnail can conceal. Its darkness is a relative condition within a
continuous photographic scale. The display must preserve that relation to
the illuminated cliff and the pale distance. A uniformly brightened version
would alter the separation of planes on which this view depends.
