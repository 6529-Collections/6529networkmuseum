# Alt text

**Poster still, on the object page beside a caption that identifies it as a still:** A rounded teal head on black, crossed by a hot-pink band; two black eyes under mint flame outlines; checkered streaks fall through the face.

**Poster still in cards, thumbnails, and social images, where no caption is present:** Still from a looping animation: a teal head on black, a hot-pink band across two black eyes under mint flames, checkered streaks falling through it.

**Animation:** Flashing animated loop of a teal head on black: its pink band flickers to magenta, mint flames boil above two black eyes, checkered streaks flash.

## Long description

A square image on a black ground, mostly filled by a large rounded head built from scraped, finely dithered teal and navy paint. Ragged black bars and small pink slashes mark the dome. A broad pink band runs across the middle of the face; along its upper edge sit three small black slots, each holding a mint bracket. On the band are two large black eyes, each crowned by a curling mint-green flame outline and crossed by a mint bar holding a small pink square with a black pupil. Between the eyes, a teal column is punched with small black squares. Below the band, loose teal blocks above a heavy teal bar form a grille-like mouth. A mint telephone handset marks the left side of the head. Thin black-and-white checkered streaks, some hooked at the top, fall through the center and right of the face and past its lower edge; one ends at upper right in a pale teal ring holding a tiny pink skull. In the animation, eight frames repeat every 0.4 seconds: the band and pink details switch between hot pink and magenta on every frame, the streaks flicker, and the flames and pupils are redrawn.

## Motion and flashing note

This work flickers continuously. Its eight frames play at 20 frames per second and alternate between two color states, so the pink band switches between hot pink and magenta twenty times a second. The black-and-white checkered streaks and the bright mint flame outlines flash against black up to ten times a second; together they cover about 5.5 percent of the image. Because parts of the image flash more than three times a second, the work does not meet WCAG 2.3.2 at any size. Whether it meets the general-flash threshold of WCAG 2.3.1 depends on how large it is shown. At 768 pixels, the most of a standard 10-degree visual field that flashes at once is about 15 percent, below the 25 percent limit; by 2,048 pixels it is about 30 percent, and at full size 54 percent. The Museum therefore shows a still first and plays the animation only on request, no larger than 768 pixels.

Sources: Museum observation of the eight composited frames and Museum flash analysis of the exactly decoded 4,000-pixel source frames, recorded in the condition and technical record `6529NM.2026.005.COND.01`, 18 October 2026 (class C). The flash figures apply the WCAG 2.2 general-flash and red-flash definitions to every pixel's eight-frame color sequence and measure the largest flashing share of a 341 × 256 pixel window, the 10-degree field on a 1,024 × 768 reference screen. They are the Museum's computation, not a certified photosensitive-epilepsy test.
