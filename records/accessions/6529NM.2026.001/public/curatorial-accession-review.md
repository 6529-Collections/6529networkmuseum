# Curatorial accession review

**Accession lot:** `6529NM.2026.001`
**Artist:** Casey REAS
**Objects:** seven works from five Art Blocks projects
**Decision:** approved for the permanent collection
**Institutional author:** 6529 Network Museum, Curatorial Department
**Decision authority:** direct Museum-authorized collection authority recorded in `6529NM.2026.001.GAA-01`

## Curatorial judgment

The 6529 Network Museum approves this seven-work gift as a coherent and important representation of Casey REAS's mature generative practice. REAS is foundational to the history the Museum is charged to preserve: as an artist, educator, and co-initiator with Ben Fry of Processing, he helped establish one of the principal environments through which artists have learned to think and work in code. His own practice has sustained a rigorous inquiry into systems, instructions, emergence, image, and behavior across software, prints, installations, moving image, machine learning, and blockchain-based generative art.

The gift is not treated as important merely because the artist is historically prominent or because the tokens were produced on Art Blocks. Its strength lies in the specific works and their relationships. Three distinct *CENTURY* outputs allow comparative study within one long-form system rather than reducing the project to a single representative still. *Pre-Process #63* and *Phototaxis #308* extend the group into different models of rule, movement, iteration, and image formation. *923 EMPTY ROOMS #713* and *Ex Nihilo (Cosmos) #248* carry the inquiry into perspectival space, continuous display, and the instability of form. Together, the seven objects provide a focused cross-section of how REAS uses executable systems to make variability, duration, and the conditions of seeing part of the work.

## Artist and historical significance

The accompanying [artist and practice profile](casey-reas-artist-practice.md) establishes the longer arc required to assess the gift responsibly. REAS's importance does not begin with NFTs. His Process works, Processing's public technical and pedagogical legacy, gallery and institutional exhibitions, software-based installations, moving-image work, and machine-learning investigations all precede and inform the donated works. The blockchain releases continue questions already central to that practice: what an artwork delegates to a system; how rules generate families of outcomes; how an image records behavior; and how software moves among source code, execution, screen, print, and institutional context.

The Museum accordingly rejects two easy but inaccurate framings. First, these objects are not simply static digital images authenticated by tokens. Their live generators, timing, dependencies, interaction, and display environment are part of their material and behavioral condition. Second, they are not a complete survey of REAS's practice. They form a concentrated accession within his recent long-form generative work and must be interpreted in relation to the earlier Process, software, installation, moving-image, and machine-learning bodies documented in the profile.

## Collection-level significance

The [collection essay](casey-reas-collection-essay.md) identifies four reasons for accessioning the group as a lot:

1. **System and variation.** The three *CENTURY* works make variation within a single generative system materially available for comparison.
2. **Behavior and duration.** *Phototaxis*, *923 EMPTY ROOMS*, and *Ex Nihilo (Cosmos)* resist treatment as final stills; observed change and documented controls make duration and execution central to their stewardship.
3. **Continuity across technical generations.** The group links REAS's long interest in instruction and autonomous behavior to Art Blocks' token hash, project script, generator, and distribution structure.
4. **Institutional responsibility.** The works require the Museum to preserve more than ownership records: source evidence, dependencies, render environments, interaction, documentation surrogates, and rights conditions must remain distinguishable and reproducible.

The group also advances the Museum's broader collection purpose. A decentralized museum should preserve the cultural and technical history of networked digital art in forms that can be independently examined. These works reward that approach because their meaning and continued intelligibility depend on relationships among artist-authored systems, platform infrastructure, blockchain state, browser execution, and visible output.

## Object-level findings

- **CENTURY #31 (`6529NM.2026.001.01`)** is accepted as a dark, segmented output in which circular and linear elements make the system's operations legible without reducing the work to a trait list. Its documented cut-and-recompose interaction is relevant to future display and conservation.
- **CENTURY #724 (`6529NM.2026.001.02`)** is accepted as a comparatively open and light output whose sparse partitions and concentrated lower field create a useful formal counterpoint to the other two *CENTURY* works.
- **CENTURY #401 (`6529NM.2026.001.03`)** is accepted as a grayscale, high-contrast output that broadens the internal comparison across palette, density, and spatial division. The grayscale description applies to the observed surrogate, not every possible live state.
- **Pre-Process #63 (`6529NM.2026.001.04`)** is accepted for its organization of repeated circular masses, axes, overlaps, and translucent sweeps, extending the accession beyond the compositional logic of *CENTURY*.
- **Phototaxis #308 (`6529NM.2026.001.05`)** is accepted as a particularly clear instance of behavioral drawing: the accumulated paths register simulated movement and iteration. The artist's technical account connects the work to a longer inquiry into machine-like agents rather than to decorative line alone.
- **923 EMPTY ROOMS #713 (`6529NM.2026.001.06`)** is accepted for its unstable room-like field and its relation to questions of generated space, perspective, and absence. The accession fixes the chain identity as project-encoded token `1000713`; source-display conventions are preserved separately.
- **Ex Nihilo (Cosmos) #248 (`6529NM.2026.001.07`)** is accepted for its continuously changing construction of line, polygonal suggestion, and darkness, and for the way its controls and ongoing display make manifestation rather than a single image the object of care.

These observations are Museum interpretations grounded in the recorded outputs and cited artist, platform, and institutional sources. They do not manufacture artist intent, aesthetic rankings, rarity claims, or valuation. The transparent collection descriptors are used for reproducible distribution study only and use no OpenSea or marketplace rarity metric.

## Scope, strengths, and limits

The accession's principal strengths are the artist's historical significance; the coherence of five related but distinct projects; the three-work comparative depth within *CENTURY*; verified object and custody identity; unusually strong source and technical documentation for an initial digital accession; and rights terms that support noncommercial exhibition, scholarship, adaptation, and preservation.

Its limits are equally explicit. The group contains no early Process print or installation, no physical component, and no representative example from every phase of REAS's practice. It should not be described as a retrospective or comprehensive survey. Its live software manifestations remain dependent on browsers and, at present, on parts of the Art Blocks serving environment. Those limits shape future collecting and conservation priorities; they do not diminish the accession's present significance.

## Decision

The curatorial case is complete. All seven objects are approved for the permanent collection, individually and as a single accession lot. The artist profile, collection essay, object records, technical review, rights review, and provenance schedule together provide the interpretive and evidentiary basis for cataloguing, exhibition, publication, education, research, and continuing conservation.
