# Curatorial accession review

**Accession lot:** `6529NM.2026.001`
**Artist:** Casey REAS
**Objects:** seven works from five Art Blocks projects
**Decision:** approved for the permanent collection
**Institutional author:** 6529 Network Museum, Curatorial Department
**Decision authority:** direct Museum-authorized collection authority recorded in `6529NM.2026.001.GAA-01`

## Curatorial judgment

The 6529 Network Museum approves this seven-work gift for the permanent collection as a coherent study of Casey REAS’s generative practice. Across software, prints, installations, moving image, machine learning, and blockchain-based work, the practice repeatedly tests systems, instructions, emergence, image, and behavior.

The case for accession rests on the works and their relationships. Three distinct *CENTURY* outputs permit sustained comparison within one long-form system. *Pre-Process #63* and *Phototaxis #308* extend the group into different models of rule, movement, iteration, and image formation. *923 EMPTY ROOMS #713* and *Ex Nihilo (Cosmos) #248* carry the inquiry into perspectival space, continuous display, and unstable form. Together, the seven objects show how Reas makes variability, duration, and the conditions of seeing integral to an executable artwork.

## Artist and historical significance

The accompanying [artist and practice profile](casey-reas-artist-practice.md) establishes the longer arc required to assess the gift responsibly. Reas's art-historical importance predates NFTs. His *Process* works, Processing's public technical and pedagogical legacy, gallery and institutional exhibitions, software-based installations, moving-image work, and machine-learning investigations all precede and inform the donated works. The blockchain releases continue questions already central to that practice: what an artwork delegates to a system; how rules generate families of outcomes; how an image records behavior; and how software moves among source code, execution, screen, print, and institutional context.

Live generators, timing, dependencies, interaction, and display environments form part of these works' material and behavioral condition. The seven objects constitute a concentrated accession within Reas's recent long-form generative work. Their interpretation draws on the earlier *Process*, software, installation, moving-image, and machine-learning bodies documented in the profile; the group remains a focused study rather than a complete survey.

## Collection-level significance

The [collection essay](casey-reas-collection-essay.md) identifies four reasons for accessioning the group as a lot:

1. **System and variation.** The three *CENTURY* works make variation within a single generative system materially available for comparison.
2. **Behavior and duration.** *Phototaxis*, *923 EMPTY ROOMS*, and *Ex Nihilo (Cosmos)* resist treatment as final stills; observed change and documented controls make duration and execution central to their stewardship.
3. **Continuity across technical generations.** The group links REAS's long interest in instruction and autonomous behavior to Art Blocks' token hash, project script, generator, and distribution structure.
4. **Institutional responsibility.** The Museum must preserve ownership records together with source evidence, dependencies, render environments, interaction, documentation surrogates, and rights conditions. Each layer must remain distinguishable and reproducible.

These seven works require the Museum to preserve networked digital art as both cultural and executable material. Their continued intelligibility depends on relationships among artist-authored systems, platform infrastructure, blockchain state, browser execution, and visible output.

## Object-level findings

- **CENTURY #31 (`6529NM.2026.001.01`)** is accepted as a dark, segmented output in which circular and linear elements make the system's operations legible without reducing the work to a trait list. Its documented cut-and-recompose interaction is relevant to future display and conservation.
- **CENTURY #724 (`6529NM.2026.001.02`)** is accepted as a comparatively open and light output whose sparse partitions and concentrated lower field create a useful formal counterpoint to the other two *CENTURY* works.
- **CENTURY #401 (`6529NM.2026.001.03`)** is accepted as a grayscale, high-contrast output that broadens the internal comparison across palette, density, and spatial division. The grayscale description applies to the observed surrogate, not every possible live state.
- **Pre-Process #63 (`6529NM.2026.001.04`)** is accepted for its organization of repeated circular masses, axes, overlaps, and translucent sweeps, extending the accession beyond the compositional logic of *CENTURY*.
- **Phototaxis #308 (`6529NM.2026.001.05`)** is accepted as a particularly clear instance of behavioral drawing: the accumulated paths register simulated movement and iteration. The artist's technical account connects the work to a longer inquiry into machine-like agents rather than to decorative line alone.
- **923 EMPTY ROOMS #713 (`6529NM.2026.001.06`)** is accepted for its unstable room-like field and its relation to questions of generated space, perspective, and absence. The accession fixes the chain identity as project-encoded token `1000713`; source-display conventions are preserved separately.
- **Ex Nihilo (Cosmos) #248 (`6529NM.2026.001.07`)** is accepted for its continuously changing construction of line, polygonal suggestion, and darkness, and for the way its controls and ongoing display make manifestation rather than a single image the object of care.

These observations are Museum interpretations grounded in the recorded outputs and cited artist, platform, and institutional sources. They make no claim to artist intent, aesthetic ranking, rarity, or valuation. Any generative-trait analysis must use the Museum's published NextGen-compatible methodology, a cited source snapshot and configuration, and a deterministic result set. OpenSea and marketplace rarity metrics are excluded.

## Scope, strengths, and limits

The accession joins five named projects in seven exact objects. Its documented strengths are the three-work comparison within *CENTURY*, verified object and custody identity, object-level source and technical records, and rights terms that support noncommercial exhibition, scholarship, adaptation, and preservation.

The group's limits shape future collecting and conservation priorities. It contains no early *Process* print or installation, no physical component, and no representative example from every phase of Reas's practice. It is a focused study rather than a retrospective or comprehensive survey. Its live software manifestations remain dependent on browsers and, at present, on parts of the Art Blocks serving environment.

## Decision

The curatorial case is complete. All seven objects are approved for the permanent collection, individually and as a single accession lot. The artist profile, collection essay, object records, technical review, rights review, and provenance schedule together provide the interpretive and evidentiary basis for cataloguing, exhibition, publication, education, research, and continuing conservation.
