## Label

No walls enclose *923 EMPTY ROOMS #713*. Its room appears through interruption: acid-green dashes stream diagonally across charcoal, then break around dark planes that imply a corner, a floor, or an opening. A bright vertical seam at the right and a low green ledge strengthen the perspective without completing it. In motion, the dashes shift and the shallow depth seems to pulse.

The token’s code combines six forms—four Pyramids, one Cargo, and one Moon—and identifies CDMX as its city and Pyramid as its primary form.[^1] An artist-defined diagram mode is documented as another view of the forms underlying the optically dense room.[^2] Reas developed the project from *An Empty Room*, commissioned by LACMA in dialogue with Victor Vasarely’s unrealized proposal for the museum’s Art and Technology program.[^3] The token belongs to a distinct work released in a six-day sequence across six Bright Moments galleries and cities. Its architecture belongs nowhere physically: it is assembled by the eye from moving line, withheld geometry, and light.

## Extended entry

*923 EMPTY ROOMS #713* causes an empty room to appear through interruption. The square is filled with short acid-green strokes, set at a common diagonal and repeated like a luminous raster. They cross a charcoal field in uneven concentrations. Broad dark interruptions cut through the pattern: a sloping void near the center, deeper black planes toward the edges, and quieter regions in which the green nearly disappears. At the right, a bright vertical seam meets a low horizontal ledge. From those few cues, the eye builds an enclosure—a corner, a receding wall, perhaps a window—although no continuous contour confirms it.

The room is therefore made from negative information. Green describes a surface only until a charcoal cut removes it. The black zones read as objects, apertures, or shadows according to what surrounds them. A diagonal band can become a wall plane; a narrow vertical can become emitted light; the lower edge can become a floor. The work exploits the learned conventions of perspective while denying their usual scaffolding. Depth is not modeled by a visible wireframe or rendered volume. It is inferred from interruptions in a field of marks.

The artist and platform document the live manifestation as an animated, browser-executed field with controls for speed, depth, line length, resolution, color channels, city presets, and diagram view.[^2] Those capabilities establish that the official still captures only one condition of an optical system. No camera needs to enter the room and no depicted object needs to cross it; changes in the line field can make an apparent enclosure sharpen or loosen. The Museum’s retained observation records changed frames after a controlled wait, but it does not preserve the screenshot bytes or support a more exact claim about which dashes, seams, or ledges moved.[^4]

The artist-defined diagram view discloses another level of the work’s notation—the elementary forms from which the less literal room is generated.[^2] Moving between the documented views exposes the distance Reas constructs between compositional ingredients and perceptual result. The Museum’s retained observation did not activate the diagram control, so this entry makes no claim about its colors, positions, or appearance for #713.[^4]

#713’s recorded code is 555536. Its six forms comprise four Pyramids, one Cargo, and one Moon; the primary form and city are Pyramid and CDMX.[^1] Across the project, six form names correspond to six cities: Sun to Tokyo, Shard to Berlin, Cargo to London, Hive to New York, Pyramid to CDMX, and Moon to Los Angeles. The associations locate each work within the project’s distributed exhibition structure, but they do not make #713 an illustration of Mexico City. Nothing in its green raster pictures a recognizable site. “CDMX” identifies one branch of a formal and presentational system released across six galleries and cities over six days: Tokyo on 14 August 2023, Berlin on 15 August, London on 16 August, New York on 17 August, Mexico City on 18 August, and Los Angeles on 19 August.[^2]

The project evolved from Reas’s *An Empty Room*, commissioned by the Los Angeles County Museum of Art for the 2023 exhibition *Coded: Art Enters the Computer Age, 1952–1982*. That earlier work engaged Victor Vasarely’s unrealized proposal for LACMA’s Art and Technology program.[^3] The lineage is exact but must not be collapsed. *An Empty Room* was the LACMA commission; *923 EMPTY ROOMS #713* is not. Reas carried questions of simulation, abstraction, and machine-controlled visual space into a separate 923-room artwork released by Art Blocks and Bright Moments.

This history places #713 in a conversation about how an abstract vocabulary can exceed an isolated picture. Vasarely imagined color and form operating architecturally and technologically; Reas works with browser execution, token-derived configuration, and a networked exhibition across multiple cities. #713’s strongest historical argument remains visible on its surface: elementary geometry becomes interference, uncertain architecture, and changing light. Perception completes what the code withholds.

The contract contains 924 invocations, numbered 0 through 923. Invocation 0 is separately coded `999999`; invocations 1 through 923 realize the project's documented combinations.[^1] #713 is invocation 713, token 1000713, within that 923-work combinatorial body.[^1] The project title, combination count, and edition size therefore describe different structures. #713 is a token-bound configuration with its own forms, channels, city relation, and controls.

Those controls extend well beyond passive playback. The controls expose the boundaries of the authored system. Reas has delimited the available operations and the system within which they occur. Their importance is phenomenological: they make it possible to test how little information is required for the room to persist, and which changes cause it to dissolve back into a field of dashes.

Within the 6529 Network Museum’s Reas accession, #713 brings simulated architecture and distributed exhibition into a group otherwise dominated by compositional cutting, behavioral traces, and continuous form. It follows *Phototaxis #308*, whose lines preserve the histories of unseen machines, by making line itself an environmental skin. It precedes *Ex Nihilo (Cosmos) #248*, where an ideal solid hovers at the edge of legibility. In #713, legibility is spatial: a room with no fixed walls, made present by the exact places where green light stops.
