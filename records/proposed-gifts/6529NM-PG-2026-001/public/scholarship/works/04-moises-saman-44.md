## Looking at the Work

An apparently young person stands close to a pale wall with the head lowered. A caged lamp
hangs above, while many dark marks interrupt the wall's surface. The frame
holds the figure and the wall in a shallow, almost frontal space. The lamp is
the clearest architectural object; its grid repeats the wall's small marks and
turns the upper corner into a measured counterweight to the body below.

The shallow space brings figure and wall into close relation, but its evidence
is difficult to interpret. The wall carries dark marks or staining; the
photograph alone cannot identify their cause. The lowered head makes the figure present without
providing a public identity. That combination of visible surface and withheld
biography is part of the work's ethical and formal condition.

The frame also divides attention between a person and a site. The figure is
small enough to be read as a figure in a room, yet the wall occupies most of
the image and refuses to become background. The photograph keeps visual
description apart from the account attached to it. Its
historical weight arrives through a caption and access history that must remain
separate from the marks themselves.

## Saman's practice and controlled access

Saman's work grew from conflict reporting into longer documentary inquiries
about memory, aftermath, and the limits of photographic explanation. The
official Magnum fine-print biography describes his early response to Balkan
war imagery, his reporting across the Middle East and elsewhere, and his full
membership in Magnum from 2014.[^1] World Press Photo records his earlier work
for Newsday, Afghanistan and Iraq reporting, and that he joined Magnum in
2010.[^2] The two dates describe different stages: joining the agency in 2010
and full membership in 2014.

The 2011 Tripoli work was made in a press environment shaped by government
management of foreign journalists. The retained TIME research excerpt describes
Saman's assignment, confinement of foreign reporters at the Rixos Hotel, and
movement under government-minder supervision; CPJ records the wider pattern of
restricted movement and attacks on the press.[^3] These accounts establish a
condition of access around the work. They do not identify the pictured house,
the person, or the cause of the wall marks.

Saman's later practice gives this photograph a further context without
rewriting its 2011 circumstances. WARM's record of *Discordia* describes a
2011–2014 project across the Arab Spring's sites, published as a 2016 book and
shown in festival contexts; its sequences deliberately carry uncertainty and
gaps in language.[^4] The relation is one of practice and publication history,
not proof that this exact frame appears in that book or exhibition.

## Caption history and safeguarding

The issuer caption makes two separate statements. One metadata passage says
that the house was targeted by Western air strikes and that one woman was
injured; it attributes that account to government officials and says the claim
was impossible to verify. A separate passage says that minders showed
journalists evidence of strikes targeting civilian areas.[^5] The second
statement concerns what the minders presented; it does not verify the first
account or establish the cause of the marks in this image.

The public description therefore names only the visible figure, wall, lamp, and
marks. It gives no name, age, biography, house identifier, weapon attribution,
or later-life account. The issuer's raw spelling is Moises Saman; this Museum
display uses Moisés Saman while retaining the raw spelling in the structured
record. Any identity, age, consent documentation, safeguarding record, or sensitive location material
belongs in restricted research and rights records, not in a public
identification exercise.

## The Work in the selected offer

Saman's token is the one selected Work identified in the project metadata as
curation `1/3`; the other four are identified as curation `2/3`
([S13](../sources/source-register.md); [S10–S14](../sources/source-register.md)). That exact split
is the observable contribution of this Work to the offer. Its more difficult
contribution is ethical: the selected five-token unit brings a vulnerable
depicted subject into a public research corpus whose caption is itself
qualified. The public page can preserve that condition without attempting to
identify the depicted person. The rationale beyond the grouping is unrecorded.
