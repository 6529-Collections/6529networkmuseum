# David Seymour

*Patrolling the border between the Negev Desert and Jordan*, 1952
Magnum 75 #127, 1/1 ERC-721
Negev, Israel
© David Seymour/Magnum Photos 2022. All Rights Reserved.

Seymour builds the photograph around a line that cannot be seen. An armed,
back-facing figure fills the lower frame, his rifle rising diagonally toward a
bright canvas shelter set deep in a valley of dark, stratified rock. His body
supplies scale and point of view: the landscape is encountered from a guarded
position. No marker in the image identifies the armistice line or the person
watching it. Border and patrol come from the caption; the photograph gives
their spatial pressure.

The David Seymour estate publishes a visually matching frame and locates it in
the King Solomon's Mines area on the Negev–Jordan border. Its public record
does not cross-index the estate filename to the Magnum archive number. In 1952
this was the unsettled armistice demarcation created by the 1949 Israel–Jordan
agreement.

Seymour, known professionally as Chim, co-founded Magnum in 1947. His postwar
work in Israel sits beside his photographs of displaced children and European
reconstruction, extending a practice attentive to political events through the
people living inside them. The commissioner, first publication, exact
assignment dates, and the identity and unit of the foreground figure remain
unestablished in the public sources reviewed here.
