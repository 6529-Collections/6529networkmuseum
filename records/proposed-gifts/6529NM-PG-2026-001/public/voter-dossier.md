## The gift

An armed figure looks across a desert border outside the frame. Two soldiers
stand in a church beneath the crucifix. A runner crosses a cloud of tear gas at
the Western Wall. A child stands before damage whose cause
reporters could not verify. A soldier sits among the remains of a temple
destroyed months before the photograph was made.

Made between 1952 and 2016 by David Seymour, Larry Towell, Micha Bar-Am, Moisés
Saman, and Lorenzo Meloni, the five photographs locate authority in a border,
a church, a sacred enclosure, a managed press visit, and an archaeological
ruin. Four were selected for Azu Nwagbogu's second Magnum 75 curation, organized
around the “indecisive moment.” Saman's photograph comes from Alejandro
Cartagena's first curation and makes uncertainty explicit: the government
presented a damaged house as evidence, while the accompanying caption withheld
verification.

The donor formed the group from five individual works. Magnum, the five
photographers, and the two original curators have made no claim for it as a
series.

# David Seymour

*Patrolling the border between the Negev Desert and Jordan*, 1952
Magnum 75 #127, 1/1 ERC-721
Negev, Israel
© David Seymour/Magnum Photos 2022. All Rights Reserved.

Seymour builds the photograph around a line that cannot be seen. An armed,
back-facing figure fills the lower frame, his rifle rising diagonally toward a
bright canvas shelter set deep in a valley of dark, stratified rock. His body
supplies scale and point of view: the landscape is encountered from a guarded
position. No marker in the image identifies the armistice line or the person
watching it. Border and patrol come from the caption; the photograph gives
their spatial pressure.

The David Seymour estate publishes a visually matching frame and locates it in
the King Solomon's Mines area on the Negev–Jordan border. Its public record
does not cross-index the estate filename to the Magnum archive number. In 1952
this was the unsettled armistice demarcation created by the 1949 Israel–Jordan
agreement.

Seymour, known professionally as Chim, co-founded Magnum in 1947. His postwar
work in Israel sits beside his photographs of displaced children and European
reconstruction, extending a practice attentive to political events through the
people living inside them. The commissioner, first publication, exact
assignment dates, and the identity and unit of the foreground figure remain
unestablished in the public sources reviewed here.

# Larry Towell

*Government soldiers in a church, Suchitoto, El Salvador*, 1986
Magnum 75 #145, 1/1 ERC-721
Suchitoto, El Salvador
© Larry Towell/Magnum Photos 2022. All Rights Reserved.

Two soldiers face the camera in the lower half of a nearly symmetrical church
interior. Their rifles angle away from their bodies as the crucifix rises
between and above them. Windows admit light from both sides; the altar flowers
are small and low. Towell sets armed presence against sacred architecture,
giving each order—military, religious, pictorial—an axis of its own.

The source caption places the church near the Guazapa guerrilla stronghold and
connects the scene to persecution of the Catholic Church during El Salvador's
civil war. That context does not identify the church, the soldiers, or their
purpose there. Their identities and unit, and the image's initial assignment
and publication, remain unestablished in the public sources reviewed here.

Within this proposed group, the photograph is the most frontal: two soldiers
face the camera, and the church's symmetry holds their presence in place. The
image leaves the scene's precise circumstances unresolved.

# Micha Bar-Am

*Demonstration, Western Wall, Jerusalem*, 1989
Magnum 75 #97, 1/1 ERC-721
Jerusalem
© Micha Bar-Am/Magnum Photos 2022. All Rights Reserved.

A figure runs from left to right through a field of smoke, one arm extended
beyond the edge of the frame. The camera catches a canister in the air. The
Western Wall enters as a band of masonry; metal menorahs and a low barrier
cross the foreground. Sacred place, police action, and bodily urgency occupy
the same shallow space.

The token caption associates the photograph with protests over women's
collective prayer at the Western Wall. Contemporary legal, government, and news
records document confrontation and police tear gas in March 1989. The precise
day, the central person's identity, and any membership in Women of the Wall
remain unresolved. “Western Wall” is used here as the current place name;
“Wailing Wall” appears in some historical collection records.

The same frame appears in public records from the Micha Bar-Am Archive, the
International Center of Photography, and Museum Wales. Together, those records
support its identification across institutions while leaving the precise date
and the central person's identity unresolved. Smoke makes the limit of
visibility literal: the event is visible while person and moment remain open.

# Moisés Saman

*Tripoli, Libya*, 2011
Magnum 75 #44, 1/1 ERC-721
Tripoli, Libya
© Moisés Saman/Magnum Photos 2022. All Rights Reserved.

A child, described in the source caption as a girl, stands against a white wall
covered by punctures and broken plaster. A caged lamp hangs above her. The
light scarf across her dark coat lifts toward the right while her head and eyes
turn down. The photograph places a living body beside marks offered as proof,
yet it withholds any visual means of assigning those marks a cause.

Libyan government officials brought foreign journalists to the house and said
Western air strikes had damaged it and injured a woman. Saman's caption states
that the claim could not be verified. The cited accounts describe
government-controlled access for foreign journalists in Tripoli. The exact
house and date, the weapon or responsible force, the reported injury, and the
identity of the child remain unresolved.

Within this proposed group, the photograph tests how a caption can preserve
uncertainty while an official account seeks to turn visible damage into
evidence. The image leaves the claim unresolved; the caption records its
limits.

# Lorenzo Meloni

*Palmyra, Syria*, 1 April 2016
Magnum 75 #104, 1/1 ERC-721
Palmyra, Syria
© Lorenzo Meloni/Magnum Photos 2022. All Rights Reserved.

Pale stone and hard sunlight spread across a wide frame. A soldier sits low in
the foreground rubble, lifting his helmet over his face. His camouflage and
rifle mark the military present; broken columns and standing portico fragments
carry a much longer history. The body is small beside the field of remains. A
gesture that might suggest exhaustion, shielding, or adjustment stays
unresolved.

UNESCO records that Islamic State destroyed the principal structure of the
Temple of Bel in August 2015. Government and allied forces seized Palmyra from
ISIL on 27 March 2016; Meloni photographed there five days later under Syrian
government permission while moving with government forces. The photograph is
situated in the aftermath of destruction and recapture. The Temple of Bel had
been destroyed months earlier, while the standing structures complicate any
account of ancient Palmyra as wholly lost.

As the latest-dated work, the photograph brings the group to a site where
military action and cultural loss meet. Its high light keeps the damaged stone
fully exposed instead of turning it into a theatrical ruin. In the
photograph's historical setting, the site is under military control.

# The five photographs together

## The case for selection

These works give the Museum a compact history of documentary photography at
the edge of authority. Seymour makes an unseen border legible through the
posture of an armed watcher. Towell turns a church into a meeting point for the
military and the sacred. Bar-Am catches religious practice and police power in
a cloud that interrupts sight. Saman records the staging of evidence and keeps
the official claim unresolved. Meloni sets one soldier's gesture against the
destruction and survival of an ancient site.

Four of the five came through Azu Nwagbogu's second Magnum 75 curation. His
phrase “indecisive moment” is useful here because these photographs leave the
histories they enter unresolved. Saman's first-curation work sharpens that
condition by naming the limits of verification. Across sixty-four years, the
group shows documentary authority as something built from image, caption,
access, archive, and later institutional use.

Magnum announced 225 planned NFTs in 2022. Evidence reviewed for this proposal
documents the first and second curations and 148 live tokens at the finalized
observation block. It does not establish that 225 tokens were minted or remain
live, or that a third curation was released. These five tokens belong to the
two documented curations.

For these works, Ethereum records provide token identity and transfer history.
The retrieved Arweave objects provide metadata and image files at the stated
observation. Each photograph's print, archive, publication, copyright, legal-
title, custody, and preservation histories remain separate.

## The countercase

The group is donor-formed, bringing five distinct practices, countries,
decades, assignments, and political conditions under one interpretation. The
argument about authority and uncertainty arrives after the photographs, and
voters may judge that it connects unlike events too smoothly.

Four photographs concern Israel, Jerusalem, Libya, or Syria; the fifth concerns
El Salvador. This concentration can deepen a study of conflict photography,
but it can also repeat a narrow visual economy in which regions are represented
principally through soldiers, damage, sacred conflict, and ruin. The group
offers no corresponding account of everyday life, resistance outside armed
frames, or the people represented beyond the event caption.

Saman's photograph places a child within an evidentiary frame. The record
cannot establish the child's identity, consent, or agency. Voters may consider
whether its display repeats the extractive logic the proposal seeks to
examine.

Several exact histories remain incomplete. Seymour's commissioner and first
publication are unknown. Towell's church and assignment remain unidentified.
Bar-Am's exact date is unresolved. Saman's official account was unverifiable.
Meloni worked under government permission. These are meaningful conditions of
the photographs, though voters may consider the record too incomplete for the
proposed grouping.

All five metadata records state **All Rights Reserved**. The available terms
convey ERC-721 control without a copyright, reproduction, publication,
display, derivative, or preservation grant. The public Arweave files are
available and fixed by hash. Museum preservation copies and a completed rights
review remain outstanding. Voters may weigh the group's documentary interest
against the limits on future reproduction and preservation.
