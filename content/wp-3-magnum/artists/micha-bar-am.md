# Micha Bar-Am

## Artist profile

**Selected Work:** [*Demonstration, Western Wall, Jerusalem*](../works/03-micha-bar-am-97.md), 1989
**Sources:** [S22–S25](../sources/source-register.md), [S34], [S44], [S50](../sources/source-register.md)

Micha Bar-Am's practice moves through war reporting, public demonstration, and
institution building. Magnum's biography records his 1948 service, work in the
Sinai and during the Six-Day War, and correspondent status from 1968.[^1] Those
phases matter for the selected photograph because the camera appears inside a
public scene where military memory, religious space, and competing claims to
access meet.

## From reportage to photographic institutions

Bar-Am photographed events in which the image itself participates in public
memory. A newspaper frame can make a demonstration immediate; an archive can
make it durable; an exhibition can place the same gesture within a longer
history of Israeli photography. His work attends to smoke, architecture,
crowds, and the way institutions decide which descriptions endure.

The Western Wall is a particularly charged setting. Women of the Wall's own
history describes a movement shaped by women's prayer, religious authority,
gender, and access to the Wall.[^2] The 1989 frame belongs to that public field,
while the exact day and identity of the person remain matters for the archive,
not visual inference.

Bar-Am also shaped the institutions through which photography is encountered.
He helped Cornell Capa establish ICP, founded the Tel Aviv Museum of Art's
photography department in 1977, and served as Senior Curator of Photography
through 1992 according to the archive release. Magnum's official profile gives
the curatorial period as 1977–1993; the archive separately dates the Enrique
Kavlin Lifetime Achievement Award to 1993.[^3] These attributed variants are
part of the biography's public record.

## Reception and catalogue language

The selected image appears across the Bar-Am Archive, ICP, and Museum Wales.
The archive uses `BAM1989009W02477-26`, token metadata uses `/26C`, and the older
Museum Wales title says “Western Wall (Wailing Wall), Jerusalem.”[^4] The
variants show how an image's public identity is built through successive
cataloguing acts. The object entry retains the smoke-and-canister observation
and its caption sources; the artist record places those details inside Bar-Am's
larger movement between reportage and institution.

## Selected Work

The selected Work is a 1989 Jerusalem photograph in the second curation. Its
role in the offer is concentrated in public space: a small moving figure,
smoke, a barrier, and a contested place-name bring Bar-Am's documentary and
institutional histories into one frame. The image's exact event and subject
remain open while its visible structure is clear.
