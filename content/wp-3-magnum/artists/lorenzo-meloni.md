# Lorenzo Meloni

## Artist profile

**Selected Work:** [*Palmyra, Syria*](../works/05-lorenzo-meloni-104.md), 2016
**Sources:** [S30–S32](../sources/source-register.md), [S41], [S48–S49](../sources/source-register.md)

Lorenzo Meloni treats conflict as a historical process. Magnum's profile
describes work across the Middle East and North Africa, a historicist approach,
and full membership in 2020.[^1] His photographs follow the rise, territorial
rule, collapse, and aftermath of Islamic State across places whose histories
exceed the news cycle.

## Project, book, and exhibition

*We Don't Say Goodbye* gathers a 2013–2019 project across Iraq, Syria, and
Libya. GOST Books describes a fragmented publication in which photographs are
interrupted by objects and writing found in war rubble.[^2] The objects interrupt
the sequence materially: history enters as fragment, label, and physical
remnant alongside the image.

Visa pour l'Image's 2017 project material places *The Collapse of the Caliphate*
across Kobanî, Palmyra, Sirte, and Mosul.[^3] Meloni's long projects use these
sites to connect ancient heritage, contemporary battle, ruined objects, and
future memory. *Kobane: The Aftermath* gives that attention to surviving
civilians and rebuilding a distinct documentary phase within the larger body
of work.[^1]

## Palmyra and the conditions of looking

UNESCO's 2015 record places the Temple of Bel's destruction on 30 August 2015.
The UNESCO Rapid Assessment and April 2016 mission report describe the later
conditions following the March 2016 change of control: the monument was viewed
from a distance, access remained constrained, and demining was incomplete.[^4]
TIME's report describes government permission and shadowing; AFP's account
describes an army minder, mine warnings, and directed entry. The retained
excerpts and page-state notes keep those access claims attributed to their
publishers.[^5]

The selected Palmyra Work places Meloni's formal restraint inside that history.
The soldier's obscured face, the helmet, and the surviving columns make the
photograph an image of a site and a pause. Its documentary force comes from the
relationship between what the frame allows and the route through which the
photographer reached the ruins.

## Selected Work

The selected Work is one of four images in the group's `2/3` curation
([S14](../sources/source-register.md); [object schedule](../machine/object-schedule.json)). It extends
the offer from street, church, and plaza into a damaged World Heritage site and
gives the group a final encounter with access, ruin, and historical scale. The
object entry owns the exact caption and manifestation record; this profile
follows the projects, books, exhibitions, and material choices that make the
frame's place in Meloni's practice intelligible.
