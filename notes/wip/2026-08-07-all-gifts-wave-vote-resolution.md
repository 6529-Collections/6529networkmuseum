# Gift-Specific TDH Approval for Every Museum Gift

## Question

**Should the 6529 Network Museum require every proposed gift, including every gift from a preapproved collection, to clear a gift-specific TDH vote in the 6529 Network Museum Wave before the Museum may formally accept it?**

If this proposal clears the applicable TDH threshold, the resolution below is adopted. If it does not clear the threshold, the Donation Acceptance Policy and the existing collection preapprovals remain unchanged.

## Resolution

### 1. Every gift requires a vote

Every proposed gift to the 6529 Network Museum must be presented for a gift-specific TDH vote in the 6529 Network Museum Wave before formal acceptance.

This requirement applies to:

1. gifts from preapproved collections;
2. gifts from collections or categories that are within the Museum's general collecting scope;
3. gifts from collections or categories that have not been preapproved;
4. gifts of one work;
5. gifts of an exact group of works; and
6. conditional gifts, restricted gifts, promised gifts, bequests, and works transferred to a Museum-associated wallet before acceptance.

The vote must identify the exact work or exact group of works under consideration. A group is one voting unit unless the proposal expressly makes the works severable and explains how partial selection will operate.

### 2. Preapproved collections remain preapproved

The existing preapprovals for Autoglyphs, Art Blocks collections, original Rare Pepes, and original CryptoPunks remain in force.

Preapproval establishes that the collection or category is already of interest to the Museum and eligible for the donation pathway. It gives potential donors confidence that the Museum is open to serious offers from that collection. It also avoids repeating a separate collection-level eligibility decision for every offer.

Preapproval does not approve a particular token, edition, object, group, donor, title history, set of conditions, or curatorial argument. The network will review each exact gift on its own merits.

### 3. Minimum proposal package

The proposer must give voters a finished public case that is proportionate to the proposed gift. The Museum Wave is the curatorial forum for the decision. The repository holds the public source edition, supporting evidence, and revision history.

At minimum, the proposal must state:

1. the donor's proposed public credit;
2. the exact voting unit and whether the group is severable;
3. the artist, title, date, collection or project, chain, contract, token ID, edition, and current owner for each work, where applicable;
4. any conditions attached to the offer;
5. the collection's current preapproval status and the decision that established it;
6. the curatorial case for accepting these exact works;
7. the strongest specific countercase for declining the gift;
8. a response to the countercase;
9. enough background on the artist, collection, project, and individual works for voters to understand the proposal;
10. the known state of provenance, token control, rights, media, metadata, technical dependencies, and preservation risk;
11. the material questions that remain open; and
12. direct sources and a link to the public repository edition, when available.

The proposal should show the works at a useful scale and distinguish visible observation, source captions, historical facts, and Museum interpretation. Scholarship for the vote should be complete enough to support an informed decision. Full accession research, condition work, rights review, preservation work, and final catalogue writing may follow selection.

### 4. Wave presentation and voting unit

Each proposed gift should appear as one multi-part Storm in the Museum Wave. The parent drop is the voting unit.

The first part should contain the exact resolution, donor credit, voting unit, and object list. The following parts should present the works and the scholarship needed for the decision. The final part should contain the case for selection, countercase, response, known rights and technical limits, sources, and the same controlling resolution.

The proposal should remain in the Wave while it is open. It should not appear among the Museum's accessions, permanent holdings, artists, or collection pages before selection and accession.

### 5. Effect of a clearing vote

If the proposal clears the applicable TDH threshold, the exact gift is selected and approved to proceed.

Identity, donor authority, legal title, rights, technical review, transfer, custody receipt, formal acceptance, preservation, and accession will follow. The clearing vote does not by itself establish any of those facts or events.

The Museum may decline formal acceptance after selection if the required review identifies a material failure, including invalid title, lack of donor authority, disputed provenance, sanctions or legal concerns, unacceptable restrictions, technical non-receivability, or a preservation burden the Museum cannot responsibly assume. The reason should be recorded publicly, subject to the proper treatment of private or legally sensitive information.

### 6. Effect of a non-clearing vote

If the proposal does not clear the applicable TDH threshold, it closes without selection and has no collection-status effect.

The outcome applies only to the exact offered gift. It does not determine the standing of the artist, collection, donor, or related works, and it does not prevent a materially different future proposal.

### 7. Transfers before selection

Donors should not transfer proposed works to the Museum before selection and transfer instructions.

A work that reaches a Museum-associated wallet before selection remains an unsolicited or provisionally held asset. Wallet custody does not create acceptance, accession, donor recognition, or permanent-collection status. The Museum may return, quarantine, ignore, or otherwise handle the asset under its policies and applicable law.

### 8. Administrative and delegated authority

No administrator, signer, maintainer, registrar, curator, committee, or delegated donation-review process may formally accept a gift without the gift-specific Wave vote required by this resolution.

Museum contributors may prepare proposals, verify public evidence, perform intake checks, identify disqualifying issues, and recommend that an incomplete or ineligible offer should not be placed before voters. These activities do not substitute for the TDH decision.

### 9. Effect on prior decisions

This resolution amends the following adopted decisions only to the extent that they permit formal acceptance of a gift without a gift-specific TDH vote:

1. Autoglyphs, Wave serial `1052148`;
2. Art Blocks, Wave serial `1052156`;
3. original Rare Pepes, Wave serial `1052401`;
4. original CryptoPunks, Wave serial `1052437`;
5. General NFT Collecting Scope, Wave serial `1052604`; and
6. 6529 Network Museum Donation Acceptance Policy, Wave serial `1052812`.

The collection-level eligibility findings, collecting scope, ordinary donation checks, refusal authority, unsolicited-transfer rules, rights requirements, provenance requirements, donor-recognition rules, valuation and tax limits, and recordkeeping requirements in those decisions remain in force.

Where the earlier decisions say that a donation may be accepted administratively, without further pre-authorization, or through a delegated review process, this resolution controls. Collection preapproval removes the need for a new collection-level eligibility decision. It does not remove the gift-specific vote.

### 10. Existing accessions and effective date

This resolution applies prospectively from the time it becomes an adopted Museum Wave decision.

It does not reopen or invalidate gifts formally accepted under the policy in force at the time, including the Casey Reas accession `6529NM.2026.001`.

Open gift proposals that already satisfy this resolution may continue under their existing Wave vote. The Magnum Photos 75 proposal, *Conflict at Its Edges*, is the first application of this model. Its outcome is determined by its own live Wave status.

## Rationale

Preapproval was designed to tell potential donors that the Museum has a serious collecting interest in certain collections. That signal remains valuable. A donor considering an Autoglyph, Art Blocks work, original Rare Pepe, or original CryptoPunk should know that the Museum has already found the collection eligible for consideration.

The experience of documenting the Casey Reas gift showed that collection eligibility does not answer the curatorial question posed by an exact gift. Even within a canonical collection, the Museum must decide why these works should enter the permanent collection, what they contribute together, what research and preservation they require, and whether the Museum can steward them responsibly.

The Magnum Photos 75 proposal demonstrates a workable public form. It identifies five exact tokens as one gift, gives the donor's reason for grouping them, provides background on Magnum Photos, Magnum 75, each photographer, and each work, and presents the case for selection, the countercase, and a response. Voters can judge the actual gift rather than a collection label or marketplace page.

Every accession creates a permanent obligation of custody, research, rights management, preservation, interpretation, and public accountability. Requiring an exact-gift vote places the curatorial decision with the network and asks donors to explain the gift at the level expected of a museum.
