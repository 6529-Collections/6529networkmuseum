# Chromie Squiggle #6529

**Artist:** Snowfro (Erick Calderon)

**Project:** *Chromie Squiggle*, Art Blocks Project 0, released 27 November 2020

**Token minted:** 15 January 2021

**Medium:** Interactive generative software; JavaScript using p5.js 1.0.0; token-specific hash and project script recorded on Ethereum; browser-rendered colour image

**Token:** Ethereum ERC-721, contract `0x059edd72cd353df5106d2b9cc5ab83a52287ac3a`, ID `6529`

**CAIP-19:** `eip155:1/erc721:0x059edd72cd353df5106d2b9cc5ab83a52287ac3a/6529`

## Label

A broad grey line bends across a white field. Closely spaced coloured marks cross parts of its surface, gathering at some curves and receding at others. In the live work, interaction changes the colours within a stable outline. The line's shape can be grasped at once; the activity across its surface rewards a slower encounter.

Snowfro's program produces individual Squiggles from a shared procedure and a recorded hash associated with each token. #6529 belongs to the Ribbed type, in which repeated marks articulate the line's surface. The 6529 Network Museum acquired it through an initiative organised by RegularDad, with funds designated for the purchase through Meme Card #550, a supplementary contribution from MadaCollects, and a sale facilitated by SquiggleDAO. The number's correspondence with the Museum's name helped the community choose this particular work.[^1]

## Extended entry

The figure is continuous, but its movement across the field is uneven. Short curves gather toward the left. Deeper descents and taller rises give the central and right-hand passages more weight, ending in a small coloured disc. Thin striations follow the turns inside the grey body. Their spacing and brightness draw attention to some passages while allowing neighbouring parts of the contour to read as solid grey. The contrast between the broad silhouette and these smaller divisions gives the line a changing internal rhythm.

The live generator adds duration to this structure. In the contributor's observed session, clicking was followed by changing colours in the striations and endpoint, while the silhouette remained visually stable. The appearance moved beyond the green, blue, and violet of the documented initial view into magenta, red, and orange. A still records a particular state of that colour relationship. It cannot convey the transition or the viewer's role in setting it in motion.[^2]

Art Blocks identifies the work as Ribbed, with Spectrum Normal. These names locate it within the project's vocabulary. The visual interest of #6529 lies in the way the grey body contains and partly obscures its colour. The repeated marks make the line's surface appear subdivided, although its outer course remains continuous. Changes of colour alter which passages attract the eye without requiring a new outline.

The program and token hash establish a relation between a shared system and an individual work. The hash supplies the values from which the program derives this token's configuration. Later rendering and interaction can disclose different states of the same work. This distinction matters when looking at a still: the token identifies more than the particular arrangement of colours captured in that image.[^3]

Calderon began Art Blocks with this project in 2020. In interviews, he connects its development to his earlier experiments with colour, light, and audience participation. The platform allowed a collector's mint to bring an individual configuration into view, while later collecting introduced further decisions about which existing works to select and how to present them.[^4] #6529 was minted in January 2021 and reached the Museum wallet on 19 October 2026.

Its acquisition adds a community-chosen association to that history. SquiggleDAO proposed this token because its number matched the Museum; RegularDad welcomed the correspondence. The number therefore belongs to the account of selection. The work's curvature, texture, and changing colour remain the basis of the encounter it offers a viewer.

An appropriate presentation should preserve that encounter's temporal dimension. In his interview with LACMA, Calderon favours a display that allows visitors to activate movement. The current Art Blocks documentation also describes recovering the project script, token hash, and p5.js dependency from Ethereum. These materials provide a basis for future rendering. The Museum's preservation and display records should identify the software environment, interaction, and presentation chosen for this object.[^5]
