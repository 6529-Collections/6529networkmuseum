## Label

*Chromie Squiggle #6529* is a single continuous line drawn by code. It enters the frame from the left, rises and falls through nineteen segments of varying amplitude, and exits to the right, tracing a path that is both mechanical and hand-familiar. The line is predominantly dark gray, but where the spectrum activates it carries brief passages of green, blue, and violet — a narrow color range steps forward through two hundred gradations, never enough to dominate the gray body, enough to signal that the Squiggle is not monochrome but a chromatic instrument held in reserve. The static image documents one state of a live generative work; the on-chain generator animates continuously, cycling through its color spectrum in the browser.[^1]

The token number, 6529, aligns with the institution's identity. The Chromie Squiggle generator treats each token hash as an independent draw from the same parameter space; the number is a sequence position, not a compositional input. But for the 6529 Network Museum the number is the acquisition. The community that governs the Museum voted through the 6529.io Main Stage to fund this purchase with the ETH proceeds of Meme Card #550, and the community voted in a dedicated wave to select this specific token. The Squiggle's identity and the institution's identity coincide in four digits.[^2][^3]

## Extended entry

The Chromie Squiggle is the founding work of Art Blocks. Snowfro released it as Project 0 on the platform's first day, and its script — a few hundred lines of p5.js — remains the simplest and most legible expression of what generative art on-chain can mean: a deterministic function of a token hash, executed in a browser, producing a unique image that anyone can verify and no one can alter. Public minting was paused early in the project's life, with intermittent mints since.[^1]

#6529 is a *Ribbed* Squiggle. The type name refers to the perpendicular hash marks that cross the main line at regular intervals, giving the Squiggle a textured, almost fabric-like surface. Where a *Solid* Squiggle presents a clean continuous stroke and a *Fuzzy* Squiggle dissolves its edges into noise, the Ribbed variant keeps its boundary sharp while filling its interior with rhythm. Nineteen segments — the arched units that compose the Squiggle's back-and-forth path — produce a moderately complex silhouette. A height of 3 places the Squiggle in the middle of the project's vertical range: neither the tight low-amplitude compression of height 1 nor the sweeping high-amplitude arcs of height 5.[^1]

The spectrum is *Normal*, the most common classification, meaning the color range spans a standard portion of the hue wheel rather than the extended *Double* or *Hyperpipe* ranges that produce rainbow-like progressions. The start color (75) and end color (190) define a forward-marching interval that moves from the green-blue region through blue into violet. With a color spread of 33 and two hundred steps between endpoints, the color transition is gradual, almost imperceptible within any single segment — visible only when the eye traces the full length of the line and notices that what began as cool green has become, by the exit point, warm purple. The *Forward* direction means the hue progresses in the positive direction of the color wheel; the Squiggle does not reverse or cycle.[^1]

What this produces is a Squiggle of quiet chromatic ambition. At first glance it reads as a dark gray line on a white field — the gray body (RGB 85, 85, 85) dominates the pixel count, and the white background leaves the image with the uncluttered clarity of a line drawing on paper. But the color accents are present: a flash of green near the lower left, a passage of blue in the center, a reach toward violet at the upper right. They do not announce themselves. They reward the sustained attention that a museum visitor gives a work after the first recognition, and they make the Squiggle a small lesson in the difference between looking and seeing.[^4]

The *Ribbed* texture compounds that lesson. The perpendicular marks function like the cross-hatching of an engraving: they give the line a body, a surface, a sense that it occupies space rather than merely traversing it. On a screen, where every mark is emitted light rather than reflected pigment, this tactile suggestion is unexpected. The Squiggle looks like something printed, then scanned, then rendered — a digital object that carries evidence of physical process within its code-born surface.

The 6529 Network Museum's collection already holds seven works by Casey Reas across five Art Blocks projects, accessioned as 6529NM.2026.001. Those works — CENTURY, Pre-Process, Phototaxis, 923 EMPTY ROOMS, and Ex Nihilo (Cosmos) — represent the curatorial, generative-art-with-intention tradition of the platform. The Chromie Squiggle represents a different foundation: the generative-art-as-signal tradition, the work that is the logo of an entire movement because it is simple enough to be recognized at thumbnail size and varied enough to reward close inspection.[^5]

This acquisition is the first purchase using Network funds in the 6529 Network Museum's history. The Museum's collection includes one prior purchase — Max Pain — acquired using donations from network members. The three other prior accessions were gifts from punk6529, accepted through the Art Blocks donation preapproval (6529NM-GOV-1052156). The Squiggle is bought with ETH raised by the community through Meme Card #550, a benefit-work card on the 6529.io Main Stage. The card was submitted on September 6, 2026, won the Main Stage vote on September 16, 2026, with 60,979,208 TDH from 81 voters, and was minted on September 18, 2026 in 310 editions. The original Main Stage submission can be viewed at [6529.io/memes/550](https://6529.io/memes/550).[^2]

The selection of which specific Squiggle to acquire was originally intended to be a community curatorial decision by TDH vote on a dedicated 6529.io wave ("Chromie Squiggle Purchase for Network Museum"), with voting open October 14–18, 2026. Community members cheesebiz and reno99 tracked secondary market listings and Gondi loan defaults, identifying candidate tokens within the 2.51 ETH budget. However, the Squiggle floor rose above the budget during the voting period, making most candidates unaffordable before the community could act on them. This required a pivot from TDH vote to coordinator decision: RegularDad, as wave admin, would select the token based on community input and available opportunities. On October 15, SquiggleDAO proposed token #6529 specifically, noting the DAO's relationship with the owner and the symbolic alignment of the token number with the institution: "Imagine the 6529 Museum gets the 6529 Squiggle." SquiggleDAO agreed to act as intermediary because the owner values privacy and will not sign documentation directly. RegularDad confirmed the acquisition budget at 2.51 ETH (card proceeds). MadaCollects volunteered to cover any shortfall, committing up to 0.5 ETH. The community confirmed a binding constraint: the Network Museum will never sell the work. On October 16, SquiggleDAO reported the owner's acceptance at 3.01 ETH, with MadaCollects's donation covering the 0.5 ETH gap between card proceeds and purchase price.[^3]

The Squiggle enters the collection through a different governance pathway than the prior three lots: *purchase* funded by community-minted ETH and voluntary donation, not *preapproved donation*. The intermediary is SquiggleDAO, which facilitated the transaction with the private owner. These differences are structural, not cosmetic: they record that the network itself acquired this work, through its own voting, minting, and voluntary contribution mechanisms, for the institution it governs.[^2][^3]

RegularDad identified the provenance opportunity early: "the combination of clean provenance and 6529 meme is so perfect." The transaction is structured to preserve that provenance — the owner sends the Squiggle directly to the museum wallet (`networkmuseum.6529.eth`), and the ETH is coordinated separately through SquiggleDAO. This avoids intermediate transfers that would complicate the on-chain custody trail. The token was transferred to the Museum's custody address on 19 October 2026 at 13:54 UTC, in transaction `0xcafeb32bd326e9e466e88eaafba67f20e968ffd53eb667ee957c503415d4abe6` (block 26227049). The owner sent the Squiggle before receiving payment, establishing trust in the transaction. The ETH payment of 3.01 ETH followed approximately one hour later (15:03 UTC) from a hot wallet for the 6529 Network Museum to the owner's designated payment wallet, a separate address from the NFT-holding wallet at the owner's request for privacy. The result is a clean two-party provenance: mint to original owner (January 2021), owner to museum (October 2026). No marketplace contracts, no intermediary custody, no resale chain.[^7]
