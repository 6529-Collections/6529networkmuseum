## Evidence key and bottom line

The exact candidate is **Moisés Saman, *Tripoli, Libya*, 2011**, Magnum Photos 75 token **#44**, Ethereum ERC-721 contract `0xe628b59d34f42b16c53f4d697f1ffd4d8d987b91`, token ID `44`, linked archive number `SAM2011005H2503/5482`. The issuer's token metadata identifies a girl beside wall marks at a house on the eastern outskirts of Tripoli and attributes the claim that Western air strikes caused the damage and injured a woman to Libyan government officials. The same metadata says that government minders took foreign journalists to the house and that the claim was impossible to verify. This is strong evidence for what the issuer recorded as the caption; it is not proof of the wall's cause, the reported injury, or the circumstances of the child.

The image itself can support only a bounded visual description: a child/girl beside a visibly punctured and damaged wall, with a hanging caged lamp and light scarf, as described in the local package. The marks are visible; the weapon, force, date, and responsible actor are not established by the image. The managed press-access context is independently corroborated by Saman's 2011 TIME account, CPJ's 2011 press-freedom report, and a UN report. A later UN Commission of Inquiry documents both regime misstatements about civilian casualties in cases it investigated and real civilian harm from some NATO strikes, but it does not resolve this house. Neither side of that broader history should be substituted for scene-specific evidence.

## 2. Visible facts and limits

**Technical observation — medium/high, pending a closed visual-observation record.** The supplied local package describes the token-linked JPEG as showing a child, captioned by the issuer as a girl, standing against a pale/white wall marked by numerous punctures and broken plaster. A caged lamp hangs above. The child wears a dark coat; a light scarf lifts toward the right while the head and gaze turn downward. These are descriptive observations of the supplied image/package, not proof of what caused the marks. The issuer's caption calls them “shrapnel marks”; that is a captioned designation, not an independent forensic determination.

The image does not establish:

- the exact date within 2011;
- the precise house or neighborhood beyond the caption's “eastern outskirts” wording;
- whether the marks came from an air strike, artillery, gunfire, fragments, or another event;
- which actor or weapon caused them;
- whether a woman was injured at that house, and if so, who she was or when;
- the child's name, age, nationality, family relationship, agency, or consent.

The child/girl designation is preserved because it is in the issuer caption. It should not be expanded into an exact age or identity. A visible person is not evidence of a model release, guardian permission, or consent to later publication.

## 4. Controlled access and Libya in 2011

**Fact — TIME, high.** TIME's 15 June 2011 *Theater of War: Inside Gaddafi's Libya* says Saman was on assignment for *The New York Times* from 26 February to 7 April 2011 and was one of the few Western photographers allowed to work in Tripoli as a regime “guest.” It says foreign journalists were confined to the Rixos Hotel, moved outside under strict government-minder supervision, and that access tightened as NATO began its bombing campaign. TIME also records that Saman sent weekly dispatches to Magnum from inside Libya and returned to Tripoli on 10 June for the assignment. Source: <https://time.com/3778127/theatre-of-war-inside-gaddafis-libya/> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC).

**Fact — CPJ, high.** CPJ's *Attacks on the Press in 2011: Libya* reports that Qaddafi invited reporters to Tripoli but restricted them to the Rixos Hotel, monitored their movements, and sought to limit reporting to the government line. It documents a broader environment of detentions, equipment confiscation, expulsions, assaults, and attacks on news facilities. Source: <https://cpj.org/2012/02/attacks-on-the-press-in-2011-libya/> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC).

**Fact — UN, high.** A UN Secretary-General briefing based on the Special Envoy's mission says foreign reporters in Tripoli described general fear, tight security-service control, and arrests/disappearances. Source: <https://press.un.org/en/2011/sgsm13475.doc.htm> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC).

**Authoritative historical context — UN Commission of Inquiry, high for the report's findings, low for this specific house.** The Commission's report records Qadhafi-government claims about civilian casualties and states that, in cases it investigated, testimony and site interviews confirmed deliberate misstatement of the extent of civilian casualties. The same report also documents civilian deaths and damage from some NATO airstrikes, including five of twenty investigated strikes causing 60 deaths and 55 injuries and two strikes damaging civilian infrastructure without an identified military target. Source: <https://www.ohchr.org/Documents/HRBodies/HRCouncil/RegularSession/Session19/A.HRC.19.68.pdf> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC). These findings establish a historically contested information environment; they do **not** identify the force responsible for the wall in #44 or validate/refute the specific reported injury.

The managed-access fact and the officials' causation claim belong in separate fields. The former is independently documented. The latter remains an attributed, unverified statement preserved by Saman's caption.

## 5. Saman's practice context

**Fact — World Press Photo, high.** World Press Photo records Saman's 1974 birth in Lima, upbringing in Barcelona, communications and sociology studies at California State University, early work at Newsday, sustained post-9/11 work in Afghanistan, Iraq, and the Middle East, later freelance work for *The New York Times*, Human Rights Watch, *Newsweek*, and *TIME*, and joining Magnum in 2010. Source: <https://www.worldpressphoto.org/moises-saman> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC).

**Artist statement — TIME, high for the statement, not a retrospective fact about #44.** In the 2016 interview about *Discordia*, Saman describes moving away from a straightforward news narrative toward an ambiguous visual narrative shaped by “lasting uncertainty,” and says that his experience made simple victim/perpetrator narratives difficult to sustain. Source: <https://time.com/4277248/anatomy-of-a-photobook-moises-samans-discordia/> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC; full-page fetch timed out in this pass, so retain the quoted/search-indexed material as a partial source until independently retrieved).

**Artist/institution statement — WARM Foundation, high.** WARM describes *Discordia* as Saman's personal journey through the Arab Spring, developed from 2011 to 2014 across Tunisia, Egypt, Libya, Iraq, Lebanon, and Syria. It says the project was published as a self-published book in 2016, with 126 photographs and three collages, and exhibited at WARM Festival 2015 in Sarajevo and later at the Bayeux Festival of War Correspondents. It also describes sequences presented without captions. Source: <https://warmfoundation.org/projects/discordia/> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC). This is practice and publication context; the page does not establish that #44 appears in the book or exhibitions.

**Artist statement — Magnum, high for the later statement.** Magnum's official page for *Glad Tidings of Benevolence* says the 2023 book explores how image and language construct competing narratives and quotes Saman on his role and power as a narrator with access to foreign publications, including the biases and limitations embedded in his work. Source: <https://store.magnumphotos.com/products/glad-tidings-of-benevolence> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC). This later Iraq project should not be used as evidence of Saman's intent for the 2011 Libya frame, but it is relevant context for a practice attentive to narrative authority and its limits.

**Institutional recognition — Pulitzer, high for the award.** The Pulitzer Prizes page records Saman's 2025 Feature Photography award for his *New Yorker* work on Sednaya prison in Syria. Source: <https://www.pulitzer.org/node/moises-saman-contributor-new-yorker> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC; the page redirected to the Pulitzer `wwwdev9` winner page during access). The award is not evidence about #44's caption, consent, or causation.

**Institutional rights/clearance context — high for Magnum's general policy, not proof of this image's status.** Magnum's licensing guidance says most images are not model-released unless that is stated at the image-screen level and says reproduction requires a license; its FAQ says representation and reproduction rights remain with authors and Magnum and that a purchased photograph is limited to private use absent authorization. Sources: <https://pro.magnumphotos.com/C.aspx?VF=MAX_29&VP3=CMS3> and <https://store.magnumphotos.com/pages/faqs> (accessed 2026-08-08 UTC). The specific image-level release field was not accessible. ## 10. Bounded object-essay prose

**Museum interpretation — bounded, not an accession rationale:**

*Tripoli, Libya* is an image of evidence before evidence is settled. A child stands inside a house at Tripoli's edge beside punctures that the caption names as shrapnel marks; the marks are visible, but the force that made them is not. The scene was made available during a regime-controlled press visit, and the issuer's caption preserves the officials' account only to withdraw its verification. Saman's restraint shifts attention from the wall as proof to the conditions under which proof is staged: a domestic surface, a vulnerable young subject, a government guide, and a photographer whose access is both enabled and constrained. The photograph should therefore be presented with its attribution and limits intact, without naming the child, turning the officials' claim into fact, or treating the 1/1 token as a transfer of image rights. This reading is a Museum interpretation; it is not a finding of accession, acceptance, title, consent, or copyright clearance.*
