# WP-3 research memo: Magnum Photos and Magnum Photos 75

Research date and access date for the sources below: 2026-08-08 (UTC). This is a bounded research memo for the proposed-gift work package. It is not an accession decision, acceptance record, copyright opinion, custody record, or finding that a planned project release was completed.

## Scope and method

This memo covers Magnum Photos as an institution and the 2022 Magnum Photos 75 NFT project. The source priority was: official Magnum institutional and project pages; archived official project and legal pages; named statements reproduced by a reputable photography-industry publication where the original project page was not available; direct token metadata; direct Ethereum block timestamps; and the local proposed-gift records.

The evidence labels used throughout are deliberately separate:

- **Fact**: a directly observable historical or present fact, or a local record field quoted as the state of that local record.
- **Official / named statement**: a claim, description, plan, interpretation, or rights statement made by Magnum, a named curator, or another named institution/person. It is not silently converted into an independently verified fact.
- **Technical observation**: an observation from token metadata, a chain transaction/block, a live API response, or a local technical record. It establishes what that source currently exposes, not necessarily the full legal or historical meaning of the event.
- **Museum interpretation**: a cautious interpretation for this repository. It is not a source fact and should not be used to fill an unresolved gap.

Confidence is attached to individual conclusions: **high**, **medium**, or **low**. “High” means the conclusion is directly supported by a primary source or deterministic technical observation; it does not mean that every surrounding legal or historical question is resolved.

## Controlled conclusions

1. **Institution.** Magnum’s official current history describes Magnum Photos as founded in 1947 by Henri Cartier-Bresson, Robert Capa, George Rodger, and David Seymour, and as a long-running artists’ cooperative. Magnum’s official submissions page says the agency is owned and operated by its Member photographers, is self-selecting, and has a staged Nominee → Associate → full Member process with member votes at annual meetings. **Confidence: high.**

2. **Project plan versus completion.** The archived official launch page announced three NFT collections for the 75th-anniversary year. It described the first collection as 76 images selected by Alejandro Cartagena and released in six drops from 28 June to 12 July 2022. Project metadata describes the broader project as three curations totaling 225 images. These are official project descriptions and plans. They do not, by themselves, establish that all three releases or all 225 tokens were actually released. **Confidence: high for what was announced; low for completion of the third release.**

3. **First and second curations.** The direct metadata for token #44 identifies it as curation 1/3; the archived first-release page lists #44, “Tripoli, Libya,” by Moisés Saman. The direct metadata for #97, #104, #127, and #145 identifies each as curation 2/3 and names Azu Nwagbogu’s dialogue as the second-curation context. **Confidence: high for the metadata relation; high for #44’s first-page listing.**

4. **Azu Nwagbogu’s stated idea.** The named curator’s statement, reproduced in Digital Camera World and reflected in the second-curation metadata, contrasts the familiar “decisive moment” tradition with an “indecisive moment”: images that hold anticipation, feeling, intent, foreboding, meaning, and multiple possible scenarios, inviting a closer look rather than presenting a revisionist history. This is Nwagbogu’s stated curatorial idea, not a Museum characterization of the photographs. **Confidence: high for the statement as reported/project-encoded; medium for the exact wording because the original second-release page was not available in the accessible archive set.**

5. **Chronology.** The #44 mint transaction is in Ethereum block 15,037,570 at 2022-06-28 02:51:06 UTC. The four second-curation target tokens were minted in blocks 16,070,265, 16,070,363, 16,070,886, and 16,071,368 between 2022-11-28 18:25:35 and 22:07:23 UTC. The industry report describing the second 75-piece collection was published 1 December 2022 and said the collection had “dropped,” with six drops running to 13 December. A mint timestamp is not silently treated as a public listing, sale, or exhibition timestamp. **Confidence: high for on-chain timestamps; medium for the release/listing chronology because Foundation’s current site is offline and the original listing history is not fully recoverable.**

6. **Third release.** The evidence establishes an announced three-curation/225-image project and directly establishes target-token metadata for curations 1 and 2. It does not establish that a third curation was publicly released, that all 225 images were minted, or that the announced six-drop schedules were completed for every collection. Do not turn the announcement or the “3/3” project description into a completion claim. **Confidence: high.**

7. **Museum boundary.** The local proposed-gift package calls the five tokens an offered exact-group gift and records the proposal as open. Its own nonclaims say there is no accession number, acceptance, or title/custody/rights transfer inferred from the offer, repository, or Wave process. The local records therefore support research and technical diligence only. **Confidence: high.**

## 1. Magnum Photos as an institution

### Facts

- The official “History of Magnum” page says Magnum Photos was founded after the Second World War by four photographers: Robert Capa, Henri Cartier-Bresson, George Rodger, and David “Chim” Seymour. It describes the agency’s early Paris and New York bases, later London and Tokyo presence, and its cooperative form.
- The official “About Magnum Photos” page describes the organization as an artists’ cooperative that has documented major events for more than 75 years and continues to represent photographers who work as journalists, artists, and storytellers.
- The official submissions page says: Magnum Photos is a cooperative agency owned and operated by its Member photographers; it is self-selecting; and only around 100 photographers have become full Members since its founding in 1947.
- The same page describes the membership progression as Nominee, Associate, and full Member. It says portfolios are reviewed at the Annual General Meeting in June, that members vote on progression, and that the full process takes at least four years: an opportunity period as Nominee, about two years as Associate, and a further two years before full membership. The page says full Members are members of the cooperative and that Associates are bound by agency rules and receive representation; Nominees have no binding commitments on either side.
- The submissions page separately identifies Estates, Correspondents, and Distribution members. This distinction matters: an estate or affiliated category should not be treated as the same governance status as a living full Member without a source that says so.

### Official / named statements

- The official history page says the cooperative model allowed photographers to retain control over their work and copyright rather than being constrained by magazine ownership. The page also names early staff, including Maria Eisner and Rita Vandivert, as supporting the photographers and the agency’s development. This is Magnum’s own institutional history statement.
- The official current About page calls the four named photographers “founders.” An official Henri Cartier-Bresson profile returned by Magnum’s own site also says that HCB founded Magnum in 1947 with Capa, Rodger, Seymour, and William Vandivert. These official pages do not present exactly the same founding roster.

### Technical observation

No technical source reviewed here establishes that Magnum Photos itself is the legal issuer, owner, copyright holder, or transferee of any of the five proposed-gift tokens. The token metadata uses Magnum branding and links to Magnum’s terms, but that is a project/metadata observation, not a finding about title or rights.

### Museum interpretation

The safest institutional description for Museum records is: **Magnum Photos is a photographer-member cooperative agency with a self-selecting, staged membership process, founded in 1947 by the four photographers named on the current official history page.** The William Vandivert reference should remain an attributed qualification, not be silently merged into the four-photographer formulation. The official pages support describing membership and agency affiliation as separate concepts from estates, correspondents, or distribution relationships. **Confidence: high for the cooperative structure; medium for the exact historical founding roster because of the official-page discrepancy.**

## 2. Magnum Photos 75 (2022)

### Announcement and first release

#### Fact

The archived official launch page is directly retrievable at the URL listed in the source register below. It is a Magnum Photos project page for the NFT gallery. The page includes a first-collection schedule of six drops from 28 June through 12 July 2022 and lists token/work #44 by Moisés Saman, “Tripoli, Libya,” 2011.

The page’s visible text contains an internal count inconsistency: it first says that the first collection contains **76 images**, then later refers to “These 75 images.” The direct token metadata for #44 likewise says the first collection involved works by 76 photographers and identifies the sequence as 44/225. This memo preserves the discrepancy rather than choosing one number without qualification.

#### Official / named statements

The archived official launch page says Magnum Photos announced three NFT collections as part of its 75th-anniversary celebrations. It says the first collection, titled Magnum 75, was revealed on Foundation on 28 June 2022, coinciding with the anniversary celebrations and the Annual General Meeting in New York. It describes six drops over two weeks, each drawing from the Magnum archive from the 1940s to the present, and says Alejandro Cartagena, a photographer and co-founder of Obscura, curated the first collection.

Cartagena’s statement on that page presents the archive as an evolving history of photography as a language. For this first move onto blockchain, he says he focused on the ingredients that make images stand out and on celebrating endurance and quality. This is Cartagena’s stated rationale, not an independent Museum analysis.

#### Technical observation

The direct Arweave metadata for #44 states:

- name: “Magnum 75 #44 by Moises Saman. Tripoli, Libya. 2011”;
- sequence: 44/225;
- curation: 1/3;
- edition: 1 of 1;
- copyright line: “Copyright © Moises Saman/Magnum Photos 2022 All Rights Reserved” with a link to Magnum’s terms.

The local proposed-gift record associates #44 with Ethereum contract `0xe628b59d34f42b16c53f4d697f1ffd4d8d987b91`, token 44, and mint transaction `0x6786...2aaf17` (full transaction hash is in the local record). ### Second release and Azu Nwagbogu’s idea

#### Fact

Digital Camera World published “Magnum Photos drops its second 75-piece NFT collection” on 1 December 2022. The article says the second series had dropped on Foundation, described 75 images, and presented a six-drop schedule over two weeks ending 13 December. It identifies Azu Nwagbogu, founder and director of the African Artists’ Foundation, as the person who curated the 75 images in dialogue with Magnum.

The accessible source is reputable industry reporting rather than the original Magnum second-release page. It should therefore be used as a named report of the release and statement, not as a substitute for a recoverable first-party release ledger.

#### Official / named statements

The article quotes Nwagbogu as framing the second curation around the familiar Cartier-Bresson “decisive moment” and what he calls the **“indecisive moment.”** His statement describes images that remain charged with feeling, intent, foreboding, and meaning; images composed in anticipation of multiple possible scenarios; and a viewing practice that asks the audience to look again and more closely. He says the selection is not meant as a revisionist history. This is Nwagbogu’s stated idea and must not be recast as a Museum claim about the objective meaning of every selected photograph.

The article also quotes Cristina de Middel, identified there as Magnum’s president, as describing the project as having one foot in the past and one in the future, bringing the archive into dialogue with new creation and exploring blockchain’s possibilities. This is a named institutional statement reported by the article.

### Third release: established and unresolved

#### Established

- The archived official first-release page announced three collections during the 75th-anniversary year.
- The direct token metadata describes the project as three curations and 225 images, and labels the target objects as sequence numbers within 225.
- The five target objects directly observed here are represented by metadata as curation 1/3 (#44) or curation 2/3 (#97, #104, #127, #145).

#### Not established

- No accessible primary source reviewed here establishes that a third curation was publicly launched.
- No accessible primary source reviewed here establishes that all 225 announced/project-described works were minted.
- The `3/3`, `225`, and “three collections” language is not completion evidence. It is an announcement or metadata description unless paired with a first-party release record and independently verifiable token set.
- Foundation’s current offline state prevents reliable recovery of the original collection pages, drop timestamps, bid histories, sale records, and listing status from Foundation itself.
- The local proposed-gift dossier’s statement that reviewed evidence establishes first and second selections and does not establish a third release remains the correct bounded conclusion. It is not evidence that no third release occurred; it is a record of the evidence gap.

**Museum interpretation:** Preserve “third release not established” as an explicit unresolved status. Do not convert the project’s announced plan, sequence numbers, or metadata count into a completion assertion.

## 4. Rights, archive, and source-access questions

1. **Copyright and IP.** Magnum’s current and archived terms say that purchasing an NFT does not acquire copyright or other intellectual property in the associated digital photographic file, and that the NFT may be displayed for personal use subject to the stated terms and smart-contract terms. This is an official terms statement. It is not a complete chain-of-title analysis and does not establish what permissions, licenses, estate arrangements, or photographer agreements exist for a particular image.

2. **Rights wording in metadata.** The target metadata carries “All Rights Reserved” copyright lines naming the photographer and Magnum Photos. This is a project metadata observation. It should remain separate from a Museum determination of display, preservation, reproduction, derivative, publication, or commercial rights.

3. **Foundation archive.** Foundation’s current official page says the platform is offline. The original collection/listing pages were not recoverable as a reliable live source during this research. The missing pages leave unresolved the exact public listing timestamps, drop-level sequence, bid/sale history, delistings, and any third-release evidence that may have existed on Foundation.

4. **Official second-release source.** The accessible second-release account used here is Digital Camera World’s report, which quotes named participants. A first-party Magnum launch page or archived official press release for the second collection should be sought if a future task needs exact first-party publication wording, the original six-drop dates, or the original collection URL.

5. **Metadata count and completion.** Project metadata says 225/three curations, while the official launch page says 76 images and also contains a later “75 images” reference. A complete token enumeration, a first-party release ledger, or a preserved platform export is needed to resolve whether “225” describes a plan, a metadata schema, a completed supply, or some combination.

6. **Founding roster.** The current official About/history pages use the four-photographer formulation. Magnum’s official HCB profile names William Vandivert in the founding sentence, while the history page names Maria Eisner and Rita Vandivert as early supporting staff. The difference should remain visible in historical notes until Magnum’s preferred formulation is established from a single authoritative institutional history source.

7. **Titles and location spellings.** #145 has “Suchitoto” in the local record and “Suchitito” in one direct metadata item. Preserve the source spelling and the local catalogue identifier; do not silently normalize the metadata without an amendment or source note.

## 6. Contradictions and unresolved questions to carry forward

1. **75 versus 76.** The official archived launch page uses both counts; the metadata uses 76 for the first collection while the project-level sequence is 225. Preserve both source formulations and do not select a final count without stronger first-party evidence.
2. **Four founders versus William Vandivert.** Official Magnum pages differ. Keep the four-photographer current-history formulation as the primary description and attribute the William Vandivert formulation separately.
3. **Announced versus actual releases.** Three collections/curations and 225 images are announced or encoded in metadata. A complete third-release and total-mint ledger remains unresolved.
4. **Release versus mint versus listing versus sale.** On-chain timestamps establish mint inclusion only. The Foundation schedule and 1 December report describe public release activity, but the unavailable platform history prevents a complete event chronology.
5. **Second-release primary source.** A first-party archived launch page or press release should be located if exact release copy, first-publication time, or drop inventory is required.
6. **Rights chain.** The terms and metadata say that NFT purchase does not transfer the photographic copyright/IP, but the exact permissions and any photographer/estate/licensor arrangements for each image remain unresolved.
7. **Preservation.** Arweave availability and matching local hashes establish retrievability/technical identity at the observation dates. They do not alone establish Museum preservation custody, a preservation license, or durable independent preservation sufficiency.
8. **Local proposal status.** The proposal is open and the Wave API still exposes a `PARTICIPATORY` drop as of 2026-08-08. This is mutable process state, not governance adoption, acceptance, accession, or title.

## Final Museum-safe formulation

Magnum Photos is a 1947-founded photographer-member cooperative with a self-selecting membership structure. In 2022 it announced Magnum Photos 75 as a three-collection NFT project. The available primary/project metadata establishes #44 as a first-curation token associated with Alejandro Cartagena’s selection and establishes #97, #104, #127, and #145 as second-curation tokens associated with Azu Nwagbogu’s stated “indecisive moment” idea. Ethereum observations establish their mint chronology. The evidence does not establish completion of a third release or all 225 planned/project-described works, and none of these findings establishes Museum acceptance, accession, custody, title, or copyright transfer.
