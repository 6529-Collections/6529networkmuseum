## Label

In *CENTURY #724*, emptiness carries the composition. A pale cream field spreads across most of the square. Along its upper edge, a broad rust-red arc has been severed and displaced; dark navy fragments collect near the bottom, while eleven thin salmon, navy, and white lines traverse the opening. Seven broad vertical slices interrupt the image, fewer than in the Museum’s other *CENTURY* works, so each displacement reads with unusual clarity.

Reas’s documented controls permit the sliced composition to be rearranged or returned to its original order, while the project description identifies the fine lines as moving.[^2] Drawing on Ellsworth Kelly’s cut-and-recombine procedure, *CENTURY* turns collage into an enduring temporal operation. Reas began the wider body in 2012; this exact Art Blocks work was released in 2021.[^2] #724 is the Museum group’s spacious counterweight. It shows that interruption need not produce congestion; a few shifted boundaries can make open space itself feel unsteady.

## Extended entry

*CENTURY #724* opens onto an expanse of cream. The field is so broad and luminous that the first reading is one of calm, but the calm does not last. A rust-red arc presses down from the top edge, broken into sections that no longer complete one another. Dark navy shapes gather lower in the square, including a compact fragment near the bottom right. Across these weighted forms run eleven fine lines in salmon, navy, cream, and white. Some cross at sharp angles; others pass almost alone through the open field. The composition has the scale of a sky chart or a pared-down cosmology, but its bodies have been shifted out of alignment.

Only seven vertical cuts organize this token’s disrupted state. Their relative breadth is crucial. In #31, sixteen narrow slices create a crowded screen of interruptions; here, each band retains enough of the underlying form to be read as a large shutter. Cream sections sit beside other cream sections with only a seam to announce the cut. Rust appears, vanishes, and resumes at a different height. The resulting discontinuities are economical rather than frantic. A small number of operations makes the eye continually test what belongs together. Open space becomes active because it carries the expectation that a displaced fragment might complete a form elsewhere.

The still’s spaciousness belongs to a composition that the artist’s documented controls can return to its original slice order, but the Museum’s retained observation did not activate that control and does not preserve evidence of the restored image.[^2][^3] Interpretation must therefore remain with what the exact still supports: a rust form too large to be contained, smaller dark fragments below it, and a gulf of cream between weighted events. The cut state distributes that disproportion. Rust is reassigned across the upper bands, cream becomes a sequence of upright intervals, and the dark lower register remains deliberately uncertain. The possibility of restoration is integral to the work; a specific restored arrangement is not asserted here.

Movement changes the field more quietly. Reas’s project description identifies the fine lines as moving while the artist-defined controls permit another slicing operation or a return to the original order.[^2] Because #724 contains so much unoccupied space, even subtle motion would become legible: a line’s passage could alter the balance between the cropped upper body and the lower fragments, while an intersection could appear and loosen. The still therefore conveys the token’s color and one cut arrangement but not the full temporal behavior documented by the artist. In execution, open space is not merely a fixed pause. It is the interval through which lines are designed to travel and across which fragments may be redistributed.

Reas’s point of departure for *CENTURY* was an operation associated with Ellsworth Kelly: create a composition, divide it into vertical columns, then recombine them.[^2] Reas preserves the intelligibility of that operation while changing its ontology. Kelly’s cut pieces are material units arranged toward a physical result. In *CENTURY*, division and recombination are procedures maintained by executable code. A given token provides a distinct composition and state, and the work continues beyond the image minted from that state. Its lines are specified to remain in motion, and its structure can be cut again. The technique that once connected preparatory collage to a finished painting becomes an ongoing relation among source image, interruption, and display.

In `#724`, the operation is unusually legible. Broad cream passages expose every seam and give small displacements unusual consequence. A rust arc shifted across a few bands can become disk, horizon, canopy, or ruin. A dark fragment can appear anchored in one state and stranded in another. These visible relations, rather than supply or feature counts, provide the ground for interpretation.

The medium is distributed across several distinct things. The code defines the operations. Token-derived data specifies this work's cream, rust, and navy composition and its line-and-slice configuration. A browser executes the instructions, and a screen gives their changing relation perceptible form. The Ethereum token records chain identity and transfer history; legal title, copyright, and Museum accession are recorded separately. The official PNG preserves a stable reference view for cataloguing and comparison; the live manifestation adds duration and control to the composition.

Within the 6529 Network Museum’s accession, #724 occupies a precise comparative role. The three *CENTURY* tokens are not an artist-designated triptych, yet together they permit sustained study of one software work through sharply different outputs. #31 is dense, blue, and occluded; #401 builds grayscale depth from opaque and translucent planes. #724 is the opening between them. Its cream field and asymmetrical pair of bodies demonstrate that the slicing procedure can intensify absence rather than fill it.

That is the token’s quiet severity. The work offers no central emblem and no stable reconstruction in the still. It asks the eye to hold a huge cropped disk, a minor dark body, moving diagonals, and a sequence of nearly empty bands in one provisional order. The cut has not destroyed the image; it has made the distance inside the image palpable. #724 turns interval into form—and makes every seam a measure of what has been moved apart.
