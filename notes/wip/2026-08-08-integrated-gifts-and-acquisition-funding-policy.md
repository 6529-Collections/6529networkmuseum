# Museum Gifts, Acquisition Programs, and Funding Assets Policy

## Question

Should the 6529 Network Museum adopt this policy for gifts of art, acquisition-program authorization, funding gifts, nonstandard assets, and unsolicited transfers?

If this proposal clears the applicable TDH threshold, the resolution below is adopted. If it does not clear the threshold, the Museum's existing adopted decisions remain unchanged.

## Resolution

### 1. Purpose

The Museum accepts art for permanent stewardship and funding for its public mission. It is not a trading operation, investment manager, market maker, or service for timing the sale of donated assets.

This policy establishes separate rules for:

1. gifts of art and collection assets;
2. acquisitions made through an authorized program;
3. unrestricted funding gifts;
4. restricted funding gifts;
5. non-approved fungible assets;
6. Meme Card and other continuing revenue designations; and
7. unsolicited transfers.

### 2. Definitions

**Art or collection asset** means an NFT, digital artwork, physical or hybrid artwork, archival object, edition, collectible, or other asset proposed for permanent Museum stewardship or curatorial treatment.

**Acquisition program** means a TDH-authorized process with a curatorial premise, funding basis, selection mechanism, eligibility rules, and accession conditions through which the Museum may acquire one or more works.

**Approved Settlement Asset** means an asset expressly listed in the Museum's current settlement-asset schedule. The initial intended schedule is native ETH, USDC, and USDT, with the exact chain and token identity fixed in the operational record before receipt. A familiar ticker alone is not sufficient identity.

**Unrestricted funding gift** means a voluntary and irrevocable contribution that the Museum may use for any lawful Museum purpose. It carries no limitation, designation, retention requirement, sale instruction, spending deadline, return right, donor approval right, acquisition instruction, or side agreement.

**Restricted funding gift** means a contribution whose use, custody, conversion, timing, recipient, artist, work, collection, medium, program, recognition, return, or other treatment is limited by the donor or by an associated agreement.

**Non-approved asset** means a fungible token, liquidity position, staking receipt, governance token, meme token, wrapped asset, bridged asset, claim, or other financial or cryptoasset that is not an Approved Settlement Asset.

**Formal acceptance** means an authorized Museum act recorded through the applicable process. Transfer to a Museum-associated wallet is not formal acceptance.

### 3. Every proposed gift of art requires an exact-gift vote

Every proposed gift of art or collection assets must clear a gift-specific TDH vote in the 6529 Network Museum Wave before formal acceptance.

This requirement applies to gifts from preapproved collections, gifts within the general collecting scope, gifts outside preapproved categories, single works, groups of works, conditional gifts, promised gifts, and bequests.

The vote must identify the exact work or exact group. A group is one voting unit unless the proposal expressly makes the works severable and explains how partial selection will operate.

Collection preapproval remains a meaningful signal that the Museum is interested in serious offers from that collection. It does not approve an exact token, edition, group, donor, title history, set of conditions, or curatorial argument.

### 4. Minimum package for an art-gift vote

The Museum Wave is the curatorial forum. The repository holds the public source edition, evidence, and revision history.

A proposed art gift must identify:

1. the exact voting unit;
2. donor credit;
3. artist, title, date, project or collection, chain, contract, token ID, edition, and current owner where applicable;
4. conditions attached to the offer;
5. preapproval status;
6. the curatorial case;
7. the strongest specific countercase;
8. a response to the countercase;
9. proportionate background on the artist, project, collection, and works;
10. known provenance, title, rights, media, metadata, technical, and preservation facts;
11. material open questions; and
12. direct sources and the repository edition when available.

A clearing vote selects the exact gift to proceed. Identity, donor authority, title, rights, technical review, transfer, custody receipt, formal acceptance, preservation, and accession still follow. A material failure in those checks can prevent final acceptance, with the reason recorded publicly where appropriate.

### 5. One curatorial authorization for a complete acquisition program

An acquisition program does not require a second curatorial vote after the network has validly authorized the program and its selection method.

A sufficient program authorization must establish, directly or through incorporated public terms:

1. that the activity is a 6529 Network Museum acquisition program;
2. its curatorial premise and eligible work;
3. its funding source, budget, price, or quantity formula;
4. its selection mechanism and voting population;
5. whether and how additional contributions may change the budget or quantity;
6. the rights and submission requirements;
7. the custody and accession pathway; and
8. the conditions under which a selected work may fail final acquisition.

Authorization may occur through Main Stage, the Museum Wave, or another expressly adopted TDH process, provided the decision and incorporated terms satisfy these requirements.

Works selected under that program proceed through the program's declared selection mechanism. The Museum does not rerun the same curatorial decision in another Wave. Registrar, title, rights, technical, payment, custody, and accession checks remain mandatory. Those checks can identify ineligibility or prevent acquisition, but they do not create a discretionary second aesthetic vote.

Keys and Gates is recognized as an already authorized Meme Card-funded acquisition program. Its selected works do not require a second Museum-wide curatorial vote. They remain subject to the program terms and to mint, availability, identity, consent, rights, title, payment, custody, technical, preservation, and accession completion.

### 6. Unrestricted gifts of Approved Settlement Assets

The Museum may accept unrestricted gifts of Approved Settlement Assets administratively without a gift-specific TDH vote.

Administrative acceptance remains subject to:

1. exact asset and chain identity;
2. donor authority and control;
3. applicable sanctions, legal, compliance, and source-of-funds review;
4. secure receiving instructions;
5. confirmation that no restriction or side agreement exists;
6. recording of the receipt and acceptance; and
7. the Museum's unrestricted right to hold, spend, or convert the asset for its mission.

The Museum may decline any funding gift. Administrative eligibility does not create an obligation to accept.

A donor's nonbinding statement of hope does not become a restriction. Before acceptance, the Museum should state in writing that the gift is unrestricted and that no promised use follows from the donor's preference.

### 7. Restricted funding gifts require prior approval

A restricted funding gift requires TDH approval before formal acceptance unless the restriction falls entirely within an already authorized acquisition program that expressly permits that contribution and specifies its effect.

Examples requiring approval include:

- ETH designated to purchase named Casey Reas works;
- funds restricted to an artist, collection, medium, or future program;
- funds that must be spent before a deadline;
- funds carrying a return right, retention requirement, sale instruction, naming right, approval right, or reporting obligation beyond ordinary public records; and
- funds that would alter the budget, purchase price, quantity, or selection result of an existing program without prior authorization.

The proposal must state the exact restriction, amount or formula, duration, consequences of non-use, return terms if any, donor recognition, and whether acceptance would compromise curatorial independence.

### 8. Meme Card collaborator and continuing-revenue designations

No person may identify the Museum wallet as a Meme Card collaborator, royalty recipient, revenue recipient, or beneficiary without prior Museum authorization.

Authorization is required because a collaborator designation can create an ongoing restricted funding stream, public association, accounting obligations, and expectations about a future acquisition program.

No additional vote is required when the designation and its exact use are already part of an authorized acquisition program. A random or unrelated submission cannot create Museum authority by naming the Museum wallet without consent.

### 9. Non-approved assets

The default procedure is for the donor to convert a non-approved asset into an Approved Settlement Asset before making the gift.

The Museum does not accept a standing responsibility to decide whether or when a non-approved asset should be held or sold. It makes no representation about value, future price, liquidity, market depth, execution quality, or the best time to convert.

An exception requires prior TDH approval of the exact asset or a separately adopted conversion program. The approving terms must state:

1. the exact asset and chain identity;
2. whether the asset will be converted or may be retained;
3. the authorized execution method and recipient Approved Settlement Asset;
4. who may execute;
5. custody, routing, slippage, liquidity, and compliance controls;
6. treatment of fees, taxes, failed execution, forks, airdrops, and residual balances;
7. whether the net proceeds are restricted or unrestricted; and
8. that no sale price, conversion time, or financial outcome is promised.

Where conversion is authorized, it should occur through a prompt, mechanical process at the first operationally reasonable opportunity. The Museum must not exercise an open-ended investment or market-timing mandate.

### 10. Unsolicited transfers

An unsolicited transfer is not an accepted gift, acquisition, accession, funding commitment, donor-recognition event, or instruction to trade.

The Museum has no obligation to hold the asset, sell it at a favorable time, return it, recognize the sender, support its market, or treat a stated condition as binding. Subject to applicable law and an authorized operational process, the Museum may ignore, hide, quarantine, return, convert, dispose of, or otherwise handle the asset.

Public wallet visibility does not create an invitation to transfer assets without intake instructions.

### 11. No trading or valuation mandate

The Museum does not manage a speculative portfolio. It does not promise to maximize the value of donated tokens, time markets, support floors, preserve exchange value, provide liquidity, or advise donors on tax or valuation.

Approved Settlement Assets are operational and acquisition resources. Any conversion authority is narrow, recorded, and directed toward Museum use rather than financial return.

### 12. Donor terms and public record

Before formal acceptance, the Museum should record as applicable:

- donor authority;
- asset identity;
- whether the gift is art, unrestricted funding, restricted funding, or a conversion gift;
- all restrictions and the absence of unrecorded side agreements;
- donor credit;
- title passage and custody;
- the governing vote or administrative authority;
- conversion terms;
- rights and reproduction terms for art;
- no tax, valuation, price, or liquidity representation; and
- the public and restricted record boundary.

The Museum should publish the decision, receipt, status, and permitted public terms while protecting legally sensitive or private information.

### 13. Authority and refusal

No administrator, signer, maintainer, registrar, curator, committee, or wallet operator may bypass the approvals required by this policy.

Museum contributors may prepare proposals, verify evidence, perform intake checks, identify disqualifying issues, and recommend against presenting an incomplete or ineligible offer. The Museum may refuse any proposed gift that creates unacceptable legal, title, rights, technical, preservation, security, reputational, financial, or curatorial risk.

### 14. Effect on prior decisions and current matters

This policy preserves the adopted collection preapprovals for Autoglyphs, Art Blocks collections, original Rare Pepes, and original CryptoPunks. It preserves their collection-level eligibility findings while requiring an exact-gift vote for proposed gifts of art.

This policy amends the Donation Acceptance Policy and related decisions only where necessary to establish the distinctions above.

It applies prospectively and does not reopen the Casey Reas accession.

The Magnum Photos 75 proposal, *Conflict at Its Edges*, remains governed by its own exact-gift vote and subsequent accession checks.

Keys and Gates does not require a second curatorial vote. Its selected-unminted works remain subject to all unfinished program, mint, acquisition, and accession requirements.

### 15. Replacement of the withdrawn gift-specific proposal

This proposal replaces the withdrawn proposal *Gift-Specific TDH Approval for Every Museum Gift*, Wave serial `1281404`, in full.

It preserves that proposal's principal rule for gifts of art while preventing unintended duplicate votes for authorized acquisition programs and creating a separate, explicit framework for unrestricted funding, restricted funding, collaborator proceeds, non-approved assets, conversion, and unsolicited transfers.

## Rationale

An art gift creates a permanent curatorial and stewardship obligation. The network should decide whether the exact work or group belongs in the Museum.

An acquisition program poses a different question. Once TDH has authorized the premise, resources, selection method, and accession conditions, repeating the same curatorial vote breaks the promised process and can contradict the people who funded or participated in it.

Funding gifts pose another question. Unrestricted ETH or approved stablecoins can support the Museum without imposing a curatorial decision. Restricted money creates an institutional commitment and therefore needs approval. Nonstandard tokens create trading, execution, compliance, and expectation risks that the Museum should avoid unless it adopts a narrow conversion mandate in advance.

These distinctions keep curatorial authority with TDH while keeping ordinary treasury administration mechanical, reviewable, and limited.
