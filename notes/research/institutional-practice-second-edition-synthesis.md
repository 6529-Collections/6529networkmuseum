# Institutional practice, second-edition research synthesis

Status: reviewed research input for public scholarship. This document records
selection and editorial judgment; it is not Museum policy and does not rank
institutions.

Research cutoff: 2026-08-04

## Research judgment

The first edition established a credible comparative method: begin with an
inspectable record, work, archive, conservation case, dataset, or publication;
separate an institution's stated ambition from demonstrated practice; retain
limits; and connect each observation to a change the 6529 Network Museum can
make in its own records.

The second edition keeps that evidence architecture and changes the field of
view. Its center is the digital artwork as a changing technical and social
arrangement: source, behavior, interface, runtime, network, display, rights,
documentation, manifestation, and intervention. Conventional collecting
museums remain important where their object records, scholarship, conservation,
or publication systems answer one of those questions. Institutions devoted to
media art, software, networks, digital preservation, and technical culture now
carry more of the argument.

## Adjacent chain-native practice

The research does not support a single category called "decentralized museum."
Feral File, Art Blocks, Bright Moments, Verse, DAM, MoRE, DiMoDA, Museum of
Other Realities, Museum of Crypto Art, Furtherfield, The Wrong Biennale,
DATALAND, and 0xCollection variously operate as platforms, galleries, archives,
festivals, communities, virtual exhibition systems, private collections,
marketplaces, or self-described museums.

Their practices still matter:

- Art Blocks exposes contract, project, script, dependency, token-hash, and
  parameter mechanics;
- Feral File joins curated exhibitions to object and protocol interfaces;
- Bright Moments documents mint and reveal as situated public events;
- Verse makes curator, nominator, contract owner, and temporary mint authority
  legible alongside a sales context;
- DAM develops artist-centered histories of computational art;
- MoRE separates collection, archive, exhibition, donation, rights, and later
  realization;
- DiMoDA and Museum of Other Realities foreground native runtime, package,
  hardware, software, and exhibition dependencies;
- Furtherfield and The Wrong document distributed programs and curatorial
  nodes;
- DATALAND publishes unusually specific data, model, sensing, retention, and
  infrastructure claims whose stated status still requires qualification;
- 0xCollection shows that intentional non-documentation can itself be a
  curatorial condition requiring an explicit record.

The public study will classify each organization by the evidenced role. A mint,
sale, exhibition, archive capture, community selection, marketplace listing,
wallet transfer, or token holding never establishes Museum accession, legal
title, rights, preservation, or curatorial authority by implication.

## Digital-art stewardship requirements

1. **Work identity.** Record significant properties, behavior, inputs,
   randomness, timing, network use, interaction, display, sound, output, and the
   relationship among source, instructions, code, token, and documentation.
2. **Components and dependencies.** Inventory source files, generated outputs,
   lockfiles, packages, fonts, assets, models, external services, domains,
   licenses, versions, hashes, retrieval dates, and restrictions.
3. **Artist knowledge.** Record an interview or questionnaire's purpose,
   participants, date, medium, consent, transcript status, restrictions,
   amendments, and preferred, acceptable, and unacceptable forms of change.
4. **Manifestations.** Give each installation, replay, migration, emulation,
   reconstruction, reinterpretation, documentary state, and fallback its own
   dated record. Name participants, environment, deviations, approvals, and
   evidence.
5. **Intervention history.** Link before-state, risk or failure, evidence,
   alternatives, authority, intervention, test, result, known difference, and
   reversibility.
6. **Preservation package.** Distinguish transfer, archival, and public packages;
   record checksums, fixity method, replicas, monitoring, recovery route, and
   last successful test.
7. **Reproducibility.** Fix an environment and inputs where the work permits;
   record expected behavior, observed behavior, independent replay, images,
   video, logs, and unresolved variance.
8. **Service exit.** Inventory accounts, APIs, CDNs, gateways, domains, remote
   assets, export formats, self-hosting paths, licenses, redirects, transition
   events, and lost features.
9. **Public and restricted records.** Publish method, status, evidence, and
   limitations without exposing credentials, personal data, restricted source,
   or rights-controlled material.
10. **Separate facts.** Work identity, token identity, legal title, custody,
    copyright, license, accession, condition, displayability, and preservation
    status remain separately evidenced assertions.

## Scholarship and sentence-level findings

Museum prose is strongest when its form matches its evidence.

- Object labels identify; object records organize; essays argue; conservation
  cases narrate evidence and intervention; archive records establish survival,
  order, scope, and loss; technical documents specify a system; policies state
  responsibility. No genre should impersonate another.
- Open with a named work, action, condition, or record. A reader should know
  what is at stake before meeting an institutional abstraction.
- Describe behavior with verbs: a frame divides, code generates, a visitor
  contributes, a service resolves, a link fails, a capture omits, an emulator
  runs. Words such as "explores" or "challenges" require an observable
  mechanism.
- Make one claim, present its evidence, and state its consequence. Institutional
  prestige and an authoritative byline cannot replace the middle step.
- Name dates, roles, components, methods, defects, versions, and sources. Delete
  adjectives that claim rigor, innovation, importance, openness, or permanence
  without showing the underlying act and record.
- Use a negative clause when it preserves a legal, technical, or epistemic
  boundary. Delete rhetorical "not this but that" constructions when two
  positive sentences can state the distinction.
- Record uncertainty as a field or exact sentence: documented, inferred,
  provisional, disputed, reconstructed, superseded, unavailable, or unknown.
  Name the missing or conflicting evidence.
- Keep claim-level links in readable prose. A source register supplies audit and
  reuse; it does not relieve the essay of citation.

## Website judgment

The study belongs in Stories & Research. It should remain discoverable without
becoming a home-page hierarchy, tourism guide, institutional logo wall, or flat
numbered directory. The overview should provide thematic reading paths, a small
contents structure, and grouped case files. Stable profile and source routes,
edition notes, exact-source links, suggested citation, and the public
contribution route remain visible.

The art collection stays primary. Institutional comparison should enter object
or artist pages only where a direct research citation serves the work.

## Evidence and revision rule

Every public claim is reconciled to a primary-source register with the page's
displayed title, date shown, source type, evidentiary use, and common access
date. Dynamic or unavailable pages receive an observation state; publication
does not imply that the Museum captured or preserved an external page.

Later factual corrections update the affected manuscript and revision history.
Changes to the Museum's own adopted policy or historical decision record follow
their separate amendment rules; a comparative research publication cannot
adopt policy by implication.
